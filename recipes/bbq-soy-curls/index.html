BBQ Soy Curls

the secret isn't more time — it's the sear

From the kitchen of Rob Logic · tested twice

BBQ Soy Curls

Serves 4 · rehydrate 10 min + slow-cook 1 hr · no dairy

Ingredients

  • 1 bag (8 oz) Butler Soy Curls
  • 2 cups broth, well seasoned (see notes)
  • 1 tbsp oil, for browning
  • 1½ cups BBQ sauce — the Carolina Gold (card 46) or your own
  • ½ tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Rehydrate the curls in warm seasoned broth, 10 min. Drain — then squeeze them dry, hard.
  2. Brown them: dry-sauté in a hot pan (or roast / air-fry) until the edges crisp and catch color.
  3. Toss the browned curls with the BBQ sauce and pepper right in the slow-cooker insert.
  4. Slow-cook on high ~1 hour. (Instant Pot slow-cook keeps them firmer — that faint steam-pressure is the trick.) Stir and serve.

don't skip it —R

why it's built this way —

Soy curls aren't meat. They're whole soybeans, cooked then dried — no collagen, nothing to slowly break down. So the "low and slow gets deeper and more tender" logic that works on a brisket just doesn't apply. What they are is a sponge: once rehydrated and sitting in sauce, they saturate fast. Most of the flavor goes in early.

So don't cook them longer

More slow-cooker time doesn't deepen the flavor — it softens them toward mushy (the opposite of the firm, meaty chew you want) and over-reduces a sugary sauce until it scorches at the edges. If you need to hold them, flip to keep-warm instead of more high heat, and add a splash of broth if the sauce tightens up.

Where the flavor actually lives

  • Season the soak. Build the broth with tamari, a few drops of liquid smoke, smoked salt, garlic + onion powder. Flavor goes in from the inside while they swell.
  • Squeeze them bone-dry. Wet curls dilute the sauce; dry ones drink it.
  • Brown before saucing. That Maillard crust adds more depth than any amount of extra cook time — it's what soy curls are famous for.
  • Make the sauce a hair thick. The curls drink some in the pot, so start thicker than you'd want on a rib.
