From the kitchen of Rob Logic · tested twice

Carolina Gold Mustard Sauce

Makes ~1½ cups · 10 min · dairy-free

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup yellow mustard
  • ¼ cup maple syrup (or honey)
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar
  • ¼ cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1 tbsp ketchup (optional — body & color)
  • 1 tbsp tamari or vegan Worcestershire
  • 1 tsp hot sauce, or a pinch of cayenne
  • ½ tsp each black pepper + garlic powder

Method

  1. Whisk everything together in a small saucepan.
  2. Simmer on low, 5–8 min — don't let it hard-boil. Scorched mustard turns bitter.
  3. Taste and adjust. That's the whole point of making your own.
  4. Cool it down; keeps a couple weeks in a jar in the fridge.

how to steer it —

That's Carolina Gold — South Carolina mustard sauce — and it's built for exactly this: yellow mustard for the tang, maple and sugar for the sweet, vinegar to keep it lively. The yellow mustard is what makes it gold; don't sub Dijon for all of it.

Dialing the sweet-tang

  • Rounder / sweeter → more maple + the brown sugar.
  • Sharper → more cider vinegar, and swap 1 tbsp of the yellow for whole-grain or Dijon — adds heat and complexity without losing the classic profile.
  • They balance each other, so nudge one at a time and taste between.

Smoke — yes, go for it

Smoke is a natural fit here: ½–1 tsp smoked paprika, a few drops of liquid smoke, or best of all a spoon of chipotle in adobo — smoke + heat + a touch of tomato, and it plays great against the mustard tang.

Coffee — skip it on this one

Coffee's depth is a dark, roasty bitterness that shines in tomato-molasses-brown-sugar sauces. This one is bright and vinegar-forward, and the mustard already brings its own bite — pile coffee on top and it muddies the clean sweet-tang instead of deepening it. For depth here, reach for a small spoon of molasses, a splash of bourbon, or the tamari / vegan Worcestershire. Save the coffee-and-molasses move for a tomato KC batch.

For the soy curls

Make it a hair thicker than you'd want on a rib — the curls are sponges and drink some of it in the pot.
