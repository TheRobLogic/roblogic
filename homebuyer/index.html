A field guide · San Diego, California

First-Time Homebuyer Money in San Diego

Every program below was checked against the administering agency's own live page on August 17, 2026, then re-checked by a second independent pass whose only job was to prove the first one wrong. What survived is here, sorted by whether you can actually get it. Sources are linked on every entry. There's a section for school-district employees, because half of what's written about "teacher homebuyer programs" describes programs that died years ago.

The number that decides everything
$97,950

That's 80% of Area Median Income for a one-person household in San Diego County (SDHC's 2026 chart, effective 5/1/2026). Almost every program on this page pivots on which side of it your gross income lands. Before anything else, nail down your exact gross annual income — and if you have dependents, note that household size moves every threshold up.

Under $97,950

Your lane: WISH ($32,837 matched grant), NACA, CalHFA's low-income rate locks, Habitat. The city's own low-income loan program is currently unfunded.

Roughly $92,700–$137,350

Your lane: SDHC Middle-Income ($40k loan + $10k grant, funds available now), MIDA (round 2 pending), plus everything income-blind.

Any income

CalHFA MyHome (limit is $259,000 — a school salary clears it), GSFA Platinum, most bank grants, and H.O.P.E. if you teach in the right place.

01

If you work for a school district

The honest summary: your employer gets you exactly one large grant (with narrow conditions), one lender category, and two small rebates. Every other "teacher program" you will find in a search is dead or marketing — they're in the kill list at the bottom with receipts.

If you're classified staff (paraeducator, special-ed assistant, aide, office, operations): H.O.P.E.'s "certified educator" who "must teach" language excludes you, and so does Good Neighbor Next Door. Your employer lane is GSFA Platinum Select — which names district staff, not just teachers — plus the CSEA / Union Plus rebate below. A classified salary also usually lands under the $97,950 line, where the real money is WISH and, outside city limits, the County's 22% program.

ConditionalGrant · no repayment

H.O.P.E. — Housing Opportunities for Public Educators

LISC San Diego · intake via Urban League of San Diego County · funded by Lennar under a Council District 5 development agreement

$40,000+ as an outright grant. LISC's own FAQ on repayment: "No strings attached." No resale restriction, no clawback.

Requirements, verbatim from LISC's guidelines: certified educator · teaches within the boundaries of San Diego City Council District 5 · employed by San Diego Unified or Poway Unified · first-time buyer · household income under 160% AMI ($146,480 for one person, per LISC's table, which governs) · counseling through the Urban League. The home can be anywhere in San Diego County — the district-5 rule is about where you teach, not where you buy.

The gate is your school's street address. SDUSD's footprint in Council District 5 is essentially the Scripps Ranch / Miramar Ranch North cluster, and Scripps Ranch itself is split between districts 5 and 6 — check your school's address in the city's district lookup, not the neighborhood name. "Certified educator" who "must teach" likely excludes classified staff; credentialed non-classroom staff (counselors, librarians, nurses) is ambiguous — ask. Funding status is published nowhere; press coverage suggests a ~$1M pool (~25 grants) with about a dozen closed by spring 2026, so it may be half spent. First-come, first-served. Call before doing anything else: (619) 266-6237 / housing@sdul.org. Ask whether it stacks with SDHC's Middle-Income program — neither page forbids it and nobody has answered it.

Sources: LISC HOPE FAQ · program guidelines · Urban League intake · participating lenders

Open now

GSFA Platinum "Select" — school employees are a named occupation

Golden State Finance Authority · statewide, works in the City of San Diego · apply through a GSFA participating lender

Up to 5.5% of the loan amount. The free part is the gift slice — up to 1.5% (about $9,750 on a $650k loan), non-repayable. The rest is a 15-year amortizing second mortgage with a real monthly payment.

GSFA's Select tier names "employees of a California accredited Private, Charter or Public School District … including school administration and staff" — which covers classified staff as well as teachers. No first-time-buyer requirement. Max first mortgage $832,750.

Date to know: August 31, 2026. A promo currently opens Select to all occupations and loan types; after it ends, your occupation still qualifies but the eligible loan-type list narrows — ask a participating lender which loan types survive for school employees before assuming. The gift is "subject to market conditions," so a lender can legitimately quote less than 1.5%. Get two quotes; the rate is set by the lender, not GSFA. San Diego County is excluded from GSFA's separate "Assist-to-Own" tier, so ignore that name.

Source: GSFA Platinum

Small money

Union rebates — pick one lane, and pick it before you talk to any agent

CSEA / Union Plus (classified staff) · CTA / Provident CU (certificated staff) · Homes for Heroes (either)

Roughly $3,000–$4,000 post-closing rebate (Union Plus: 25% of your agent's gross commission; Homes for Heroes: ~$700 per $100k of price), or a 0.125% rate discount (CTA/Provident — realistically worth under $500/yr).

Sequencing trap: Union Plus requires registering with its program before you sign with any agent — sign first and you forfeit it permanently. It and Homes for Heroes both require using their network agent, so they don't stack with each other. These are rebate checks that arrive after closing; they don't help you assemble cash to close. Never let a small rebate pick your lender — the SDHC and H.O.P.E. participating-lender lists are where the real money is.

Sources: Union Plus Real Estate Rewards · CSEA · CTA / Provident · Homes for Heroes

SDHC Middle-Income First-Time Homebuyer Program

San Diego Housing Commission · City of San Diego only (ZIP codes beginning 921)

$40,000 deferred down-payment loan (4% simple interest — no payments years 1–5, then it amortizes into a fixed monthly payment over 120 months) + $10,000 closing-cost grant.

For incomes between 80% and 150% AMI — posted as $92,700–$137,350 for one person. Max purchase price $1,250,000. Minimum 640 credit score, 3% of price from your own funds, two years' work history, no home owned in the last three years, SDHC participating lender, homebuyer education plus one-on-one counseling.

Three honest warnings. (1) The pool is one $3.75M Wells Fargo Foundation grant — roughly 75 households citywide, launched December 2025, with no published remaining balance. It can flip to "No Funding Available" without notice, exactly as the sibling low-income program already did. (2) SDHC's web page and its guidelines PDF disagree on details — income floor ($92,700 vs $97,950), credit floor (640 vs 660), minimum down (3% vs 1.5%), and whether the $10k grant is forgivable after 3 years (the forgiveness language is not on the live program page). Confirm every term by phone before relying on it: (619) 578-7788. (3) The $40,000 is not free money — a real second payment starts in year 6. It also cannot be combined with SDHC's other homebuyer programs, including the below-market units below.

Sources: SDHC program page · SDHC program list with live funding status · guidelines PDF

Watch the MLS

SDHC Affordable For-Sale Housing — deed-restricted below-market units

San Diego Housing Commission · 257 units across six developments

A below-market purchase price on a specific unit — the discount is set by each unit's deed-restriction formula. On a North Park condo the spread can plausibly run six figures, in exchange for a formula-capped resale price (you give up market appreciation).

Developments: Bella Rosa, Cristamar, Sycamore Walk (Black Mountain Ranch) · Airoso (Pacific Highlands Ranch) · La Boheme, Renaissance (North Park). Each carries its own AMI tier, 60% up to 120% — your qualifying band changes unit by unit. Must have lived and worked in San Diego County for 2+ years, no home owned in 3 years, homebuyer education.

There is no waitlist and no notification system — confirmed, not assumed. The only path is a standing search on MLS/Zillow/Redfin for "deed restricted" in those six developments, with your pre-approval and education certificate already in hand, because SDHC approves the first eligible applicant. Quirk worth knowing: on SDHC's chart, 80% AMI ($97,950) is a higher dollar figure than 100% AMI ($91,650) for one person, because the sub-80% rows carry a HUD high-housing-cost adjustment — don't eyeball the table and misjudge your tier. Mutually exclusive with the Middle-Income program above; if you're under 80% AMI this is the only city-administered path currently open at all.

Source: SDHC Affordable For-Sale Housing · 2026 AMI chart

03

The CalHFA stack (statewide, works in the city)

CalHFA is the state's housing finance agency. Its help comes as deferred "silent second" loans stacked behind a CalHFA first mortgage — nothing is forgiven, but nothing is due monthly either. Income limit for San Diego County: $259,000, which any school salary clears. All of it requires the eHome 8-hour course ($100) plus one-on-one follow-up — the only online course CalHFA accepts.

Open now

MyHome Assistance

CalHFA · through a CalHFA-approved lender, paired with a CalHFA first mortgage

3.5% of price on FHA (3% on conventional/VA/USDA) as a deferred junior loan at 1% simple interest, due when you sell, refinance, or pay off the first. On a $700k purchase: about $24,500 — which happens to exactly cover FHA's 3.5% minimum down payment.

Credit/DTI floors bind harder than income: FHA 640 at 45% DTI (700 for 50%), conventional 680 (660 floor for the low-income rate). If you're under 80% AMI (~$105k by Fannie Mae's address-based lookup — this is a different 80% line than SDHC's), you get a discounted "Low Income" rate lock and cheaper mortgage insurance on the conventional side. A one-year home warranty is required on any resale property — a real cost nobody mentions. Loans over $832,750 trigger a high-balance fee.

Sources: MyHome · live rate sheet · income limits PDF

Do the math

MyAccess + CalPLUS Access — the stackable extra ~2.5%

CalHFA · requires a CalPLUS Access first mortgage · MyHome comes with it, mandatory

About $16,900 more (2.5% of the loan) on a $700k FHA purchase, usable for down payment or closing costs, on top of MyHome's $24,500 — roughly $41,400 combined. The two stack; anyone who tells you it's either/or is wrong.

The price is the rate: CalPLUS Access FHA runs 7.000% against plain CalHFA FHA at 6.250%. That 0.75-point premium costs ~$5,000/year on a $675k loan, so it breaks even a bit past year three and costs you after — only take it if you genuinely can't close without the extra cash. If you're under 80% AMI, avoid the conventional Access route (it forfeits the low-income rate lock); FHA gives up nothing. The related "ZIP" closing-cost program at the same rate is strictly worse than Access at the 2% tier — skip it unless a lender can show otherwise on paper.

Sources: CalPLUS + MyAccess · live rate sheet · program matrix

04

Matched savings — the quiet grants

$13M funded for 2026≤ 80% AMI

WISH — Workforce Initiative Subsidy for Homeownership

Federal Home Loan Bank of San Francisco · delivered only through participating member banks and credit unions

$32,837 maximum grant at a 4-to-1 match — meaning you bring $8,209 of your own money (savings, gift funds, or sweat equity) and the program quadruples it. First-time buyers at or below 80% AMI ($97,950, one person). Homebuyer counseling required.

You can't apply to FHLBank — only through a member lender that holds 2026 allocation, and members run out individually even while the program is open. Ask lenders "do you hold 2026 WISH allocation?" before choosing one; picking the wrong lender silently forfeits $32,837. No participating member is San Diego-headquartered — the statewide members (Golden 1, Patelco, Mechanics Bank, Kinecta, KeyPoint, University CU) are the leads; neither Cal Coast nor SDCCU is on the 2026 list. There's a retention period with repayment rules if you sell or refinance early. Member enrollment window runs through 3/12/2027.

Sources: FHLBank SF — WISH · participating members

Round 2 pending80–140% AMI

MIDA — Middle-Income Downpayment Assistance

Federal Home Loan Bank of San Francisco · same member-bank delivery as WISH

Up to $40,000 grant against a $10,000 minimum contribution of your own, for incomes 80.01%–140% AMI — the WISH sibling for people above the $97,950 line.

Round 1's $8M for 2026 is exhausted — FHLBank is "no longer accepting new requests." A second $5M round "will launch soon," undated. The move: get on a member lender's list now so you're queued when it opens.

Source: FHLBank SF — MIDA

05

NACA — the no-down-payment mortgage

Open now

NACA "Best in America" Mortgage

Neighborhood Assistance Corporation of America · nonprofit · no San Diego office (remote counseling, or Hawthorne / Upland / Sacramento)

No down payment, no closing costs, no fees, no PMI, and a below-market rate — 5.75% 30-year for "Priority Members" as of 8/17/2026 (the rate sheet is dated daily). A single school-district salary is below the San Diego MSA median of $130,900, which makes you a Priority Member — the favorable rate and the wider purchase geography.

The real trade-off nobody mentions: NACA is not on SDHC's participating-lender list, and SDHC requires its lenders — so going NACA forecloses the $50,000 SDHC Middle-Income package. That's the decision, not the volunteer requirement. Also: your purchase price is capped by NACA's own affordability math (which binds hard on one income in San Diego), and the advertised "21 days to close" counts from contract, not from starting the counseling pipeline, which has no published duration.

Sources: NACA purchase program · area eligibility

06

Bank grants tied to where you buy (or where you live now)

These exist because banks have federal reinvestment obligations in specific census tracts. Eligibility is address-by-address — run the checkers per candidate property. All of them require getting your mortgage from that bank, so the grant is only a win if the loan terms are also competitive.

SD county eligible

Bank of America — America's Home Grant

$7,500 lender credit toward closing costs (title, recording, rate buydown). No first-time requirement, no education requirement. San Diego County is in the footprint.

An unpublished income cap applies — BofA prints no number anywhere, so ask a loan officer for the San Diego figure before counting on it. Don't confuse this with BofA's separate $10,000 "Down Payment Grant" — that one is Bay Area counties only, no matter what the listicles say. Check any specific address in BofA's Down Payment Center.

Source: BofA Down Payment Center

Tract-dependent

Chase Homebuyer Grant

$5,000 in eligible San Diego census tracts (tested live: a City Heights address qualifies). No income limit, no first-time requirement. Applied to rate buydown first, then fees, then down payment.

Eligibility is per-address, not per-neighborhood — run each candidate through Chase's finder. Know that the finder is a third-party widget Chase itself disclaims, and it is not a complete list of assistance programs — it missed every CalHFA, SDHC, and GSFA program when tested. Use it for the Chase grant only.

Sources: Chase Homebuyer Grant · address checker

Two income tiers

Wells Fargo — Homebuyer Access Grant (+ closing credit)

$10,000 toward down payment (income up to $392,700), plus a $5,000 closing-cost credit only if income is at or below $104,720 — that split was confirmed against Wells Fargo's own eligibility system, and most write-ups miss it.

Fixed-rate conventional loans only — no FHA, which is often the cheaper first-time path, so price the whole loan, not the grant. Eligibility can be satisfied by either your current address or the property being in an eligible area. The grant is taxable (1099-MISC) and Wells Fargo's own footnote warns it can affect income-based programs — including income-driven student-loan repayment, which is a real consideration on a school salary.

Sources: Wells Fargo affordable options

Gated on current address

U.S. Bank — Access Home Loan

$5,000 non-repayable credit + up to $12,500 deferred (repayable) assistance + U.S. Bank pays the mortgage insurance.

The gate runs backwards from every other program: your current residence must be in a majority-minority census tract in the San Diego area — the home you buy can be almost anywhere. Two-minute check: geocode your current address at the FFIEC map; if the tract is under 50% minority population, this program is off the table and no application effort changes that. Don't count the $12,500 as free money in comparisons — it's a lien due when you sell or refinance. 640 credit, 43% DTI ceiling, $1,000 minimum of your own, education course.

Sources: U.S. Bank Access Home Loan · FFIEC geocoder

Likely, confirm

Citi — Lender Paid Assistance

$7,500 non-repayable credit toward closing costs, potentially the largest closing credit here. Works with any loan type including FHA. Requires income under 120% of area median or a property in a low-to-moderate-income tract, plus a course from a Citi-approved counseling agency.

Available in Citi "assessment areas" — defined by branch presence. San Diego has six Citibank branches, so it's likely in-footprint, but that final yes/no needs one phone call. Can't be combined with any other Citi incentive (including their depositor rate discounts) — ask which is worth more.

Sources: Citi Lender Paid Assistance · Citi HomeRun (3% down, no MI)

07

Only if you buy outside city limits

These are real and mostly funded — and unavailable inside the City of San Diego. They're here because SDHC administers several of them from the same web page as the city programs, and misreading a county row as a city option is the easiest mistake on that page.

Not city of SDFunded

County of San Diego DCCA — the 22% second

Up to 22% of purchase price as a deferred loan (3% simple) + 4%/$10,000 closing grant, price cap $743,000, income ≤ 80% AMI. The moderate tier (80–120% AMI) is 17% with no grant. If you'd consider La Mesa, Lemon Grove, Santee, Poway, Vista, or the unincorporated county, this is the strongest deferred loan in the region.

Repayment triggers include one that surprises people: paying off your first mortgage early calls the whole loan due. Tenant-occupied properties are ineligible at offer time, no exceptions; termite clearance can't be waived; pre-1979 homes need lead clearance before close (confirm those last three with SDHC — sourced from a guidelines PDF). The two tiers cover different city lists — check yours.

Sources: County DCCA · SDHC status page

Chula VistaFunded

Chula Vista First-Time Homebuyer Program

Up to $120,000 (22% of price) deferred at 3% simple — the biggest cash-at-closing figure in the region. Income ≤ 80% AMI, price cap $807,500.

Throughput is tiny — roughly 15 families served total (with El Cajon) since 2022, so "Funds Available" doesn't mean fast or likely. The full amount accrues interest for up to 30 years; model the payoff before celebrating the headline number.

Source: SDHC (administers for Chula Vista)

Other citiesCall to confirm

Escondido HELP · El Cajon "American Dream" · Oceanside · National City

Escondido: ~5% loan (you must add 1% of your own) — new fiscal-year funding unconfirmed, (760) 839-4841. El Cajon: shared-appreciation loan with conflicting published caps ($150k/$100k vs $100k/$50k — assume the lower until SDHC confirms, (619) 578-7302). Oceanside and National City: program pages are behind bot-walls; nothing about current terms could be verified by anyone. Treat all four as phone calls, not plans.

On any shared-appreciation loan ("no interest!"): you repay a share of your home's price growth instead. In a rising market that routinely costs more than a 3%-simple loan of the same size. Get the equity-share formula in writing before comparing.

Sources: Escondido HOME · El Cajon housing division

08

Watch list — real, but nothing to get today

Portal closed

CalHFA Dream For All (the 20% shared-appreciation loan)

Up to 20% of price capped at $150,000 — but the application portal is closed ("the portal is closed," per CalHFA's own FAQ), the waitlist takes no newcomers, and the May 2026 voucher release went to people who had already applied.

Two things decide whether to ever track this again. First: it requires being a first-generation buyer — your parents can never have owned a home (or you spent time in foster care). Settle that question once; if it fails, drop this program permanently. Second: it's a randomized lottery, and repayment is the loan plus 20% of your home's appreciation — in San Diego that can be the most expensive "assistance" on this page. Ignore any 2026-dated article describing it in present tense; those were written during the Feb–Mar window.

Source: CalHFA Dream For All

Unfunded

SDHC Low-Income First-Time Homebuyer loan (the 17% / $125,000 second)

The richest in-city program when alive — 17% of price up to $125,000 deferred at 3% simple, plus a $10,000 grant, for incomes under 80% AMI. Status today, verbatim from SDHC's page: "No Funding Available." No waitlist, no reopening date.

Its full terms are still published right next to the funding flag, which is exactly how buyers (and some agents) come away believing it's live. Ask SDHC to notify you if it refunds; spend zero effort otherwise.

Source: SDHC funding-status page

Allocation dry

Mortgage Credit Certificates (MCC) — both doors closed

A federal tax credit worth 20% of your mortgage interest every year — roughly $8,900 in year one on a typical San Diego loan, uncapped (the $2,000 IRS cap only applies to rates above 20%). Which is why it's worth watching: GSFA's program has been "Fully Allocated" since January 2025, and the old regional MCC's website is literally a parked domain.

If a lender promises you an MCC, make them name the issuer and produce an open allocation in writing. If it ever reopens: it's only worth your actual federal tax liability, and selling within 9 years can claw some back.

Sources: GSFA MCC status · 26 USC §25 (the 20% cap rule)

Background

Habitat for Humanity San Diego · HUD Good Neighbor Next Door · Prop 37

All real, none actionable. Habitat: income $61,250–$97,950, affordable mortgage at ~30% of income — but "No homeownership orientations are scheduled at this time," 250 personal sweat-equity hours, and builds currently skew to National City. Good Neighbor Next Door: 50% off HUD homes for teachers — but you must teach in the home's locality, classified staff don't qualify, and San Diego inventory rounds to zero (checks returned no properties statewide). Prop 37 (Nov 2026 ballot): would create 17% state seconds — for newly constructed homes only, which mostly aren't where first-time buyers shop, and nothing before 2027 even if it passes.

Sources: Habitat SD · HUD GNND · HUD Homestore · LAO Prop 37 analysis

Dead, defunct, or bait

Everything below still ranks in search results, gets quoted by agents, or appears in bank tools. None of it will put a dollar in your hands. This list is half the value of this page.

CalHFA "School Teacher and Employee" program (STEAP) / Extra Credit Teacher program

Discontinued effective March 2, 2020 — CalHFA's own Program Bulletin #2020-01 says so in writing, and the program page is a 404 on CalHFA's server. It's still the top search result for "California teacher home buying program," with 2026-dated blogs describing it as live. There is no CalHFA educator benefit today, for teachers or classified staff — school employees get exactly what every other first-time buyer gets. Any lender who pitches STEAP is reading SEO spam; treat the rest of their program list accordingly.

Source: CalHFA Bulletin #2020-01 (the discontinuation notice) · current program list

CalSTRS Home Loan Program · CalPERS Member Home Loan

Dead since 2011–2012 and December 2010 respectively, per both systems' own publications. CalPERS explicitly warns that companies advertising "CalPERS home loans" are unaffiliated marketers — and school employees are their target audience.

Sources: CalSTRS FAQ · CalPERS newsroom

Landed (the educator down-payment company)

Direct-to-consumer business discontinued, down-payment program "on pause until further notice" per Landed's own page. It now only serves employer partners, and SDUSD isn't one.

Source: Landed

"Teacher Next Door" (teachernextdoor.us)

A private real-estate brokerage, not a government program. Its advertised "grants up to $9,000" come with its own disclaimer that amounts are estimates that may be "limited, restricted, or unavailable," and no funding source is named anywhere on the site. What you actually get is a buyer's agent you didn't choose. The government programs it leans on are the dead ones above.

Source: their own California page

San Diego Foundation "Access Granted" ($40,000 grant)

"At this time, this program has run out of funding" — LISC's own page. Notable because Bank of America's Down Payment Center still lists it with no caveat. Standing rule this page runs on: trust the administering organization, never a bank tool or aggregator. Ask the Urban League to flag you if it refunds.

Source: LISC program guidelines

SDUSD employee housing (University Heights, etc.)

Real, big (~2,300+ units planned), and rental only — nothing is for sale to employees, nothing is built, and occupancy is years out. The January 2026 board action authorized negotiations, not construction. Headlines about it read like homebuying help for teachers; it isn't.

Sources: SDUSD newsroom · Voice of San Diego

"Grants for women homebuyers"

The category doesn't exist. Government assistance can't be sex-restricted (Fair Housing Act), and no San Diego nonprofit offers a women-only purchase grant — the pages that rank for this phrase are lead-generation funnels for mortgage brokers. The characteristics that actually carry money here: income relative to $97,950, household size, first-time status, and address. Those are the levers; spend the search time there.

Checked against: SDHC · San Diego Foundation's own program roundup

Also gone or not-for-you

San Diego Community Land Trust — defunct; domain expired and in redemption. UCSD Mortgage Origination Program — UC ladder-rank faculty only. BofA $10,000 Down Payment Grant — Bay Area counties only. County "Assist-to-Own" — doesn't exist on the County's site. SDEA / CFT member housing benefits — none exist (SDEA matters only as your path to CTA membership). Order of operations

  1. Pin down exact gross income against the $97,950 line — and ask SDHC which year's AMI chart lenders are currently applying, since SDHC's own pages disagree. This one number routes everything else.
  2. If you're a credentialed teacher: check your school's address against the Council District 5 map and call the Urban League about H.O.P.E. today — (619) 266-6237. It's first-come and possibly half spent. Classified staff: skip H.O.P.E.; your calls are a GSFA Platinum lender, the Union Plus registration decision, and — if you're under the income line — WISH allocation.
  3. Before talking to any agent: decide on one rebate lane (Union Plus requires registering first; it and Homes for Heroes can't stack).
  4. Before picking a lender: if under 80% AMI, ask who holds 2026 WISH allocation. Cross-reference SDHC's participating-lender list, H.O.P.E.'s lender list, and CalHFA-approved officers — the lender who can originate your whole stack is the right one. This is also where NACA is decided: its terms are excellent, but it forecloses the SDHC package.
  5. Start the education requirements early — eHome ($100) for anything CalHFA; SDHC's course + counseling for SDHC programs. Certificates in hand make you fast, and the deed-restricted units go to whoever's fast.
  6. Per candidate property: run the address through the FFIEC geocoder (also geocode your current address once, for U.S. Bank), BofA's checker, and Chase's finder.
  7. Decide the mutual exclusions on paper, not vibes: NACA vs. SDHC package · deed-restricted unit vs. Middle-Income loan · one agent-rebate program · Access rate premium vs. plain CalHFA. Every "free" dollar here has a price somewhere; the math is short, do it.
