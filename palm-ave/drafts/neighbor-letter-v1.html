Purpose. Step 1 of a three-step sequence: (1) this letter to the Salamehs, (2) formal complaint to the City of La Mesa (v6), (3) in-person meeting with City staff. The goal of step 1 is voluntary cure where possible and a paper trail of good-faith private outreach either way.

Tactical constraints (baked into this draft; do NOT add anything from the "avoid" list before sending):

  • Tone: plain-English neighbor letter. No legal register, no code citations, no "pursuant to," no underlined headers.
  • Order of concerns: drainage → grading/fence → lights → sewage. Not the engineering order — the diplomacy order. Sewage last because it's the most inflammatory and it's phrased as pure observation.
  • Face-saving frame: attributes conditions to "end of a big construction project" / "details that get missed," not to Salameh personally. Sophisticated ego-invested recipients cure when they don't have to admit fault, and dig in when they do.
  • No hard deadline. Soft "next couple of weeks" window. A named date triggers lawyer review and stall-past-date games.
  • No threat of escalation. Silent on the city complaint. Includes the magic paper-trail sentence ("welcome your inspection and a conversation before either of us has to involve outside parties") — that line is what makes this letter Exhibit A for good faith if we ever have to prove it.
  • No Mendoza payload. No dollar figures. No "unless you do X by Y, I'll report to the City / County / Water Board." Any report-or-pay coupling is civil extortion as a matter of law under Mendoza v. Hamzeh, 215 Cal.App.4th 799 (2013) — Rob's own letter hands Salameh an anti-SLAPP-immune cross-complaint. Kept out.
  • Sewage grievance as observation, not conclusion. "Looked and smelled like," "from the direction of," "I'm not claiming to know the source." If the source turns out to be the shared municipal lateral or Rob's own line, the letter is defensible; a bare accusation would be defamation per se against a professional landlord.
  • No references to: Salameh's other properties, LLCs, tenants, prior litigation, the 2022 San Diego cease-and-desist, the permit record, photographs in Rob's custody, consultation with counsel, or any dollar amount. Each of those either converts the letter into a threat, invites a §527.6 harassment filing, or creates a specific-performance demand that needs a surveyor Rob hasn't retained.
  • Fence / §841 cost-share: deliberately not raised here. The statutory 30-day §841(b)(2) notice is a separate document on a separate track, to follow v3 of the complaint letter.

Dear Bill and Lana,

Congratulations on wrapping up the construction — that was a long road and it must feel good to have the project behind you. I wanted to put a few things in writing before too much more time passes, so we can be on the same page about some conditions along our shared property line that I'm hoping we can work through together. I figured a short letter would be easier than trying to catch you in the driveway — this gives you a chance to look each item over on your own time.

1. Water flow along the property line.

After the storm on [DATE], I took on a fair amount of water in my basement, and a mature orange tree along the fence line came down in saturated soil. I'd like to understand what the finished grade looks like from your side and whether water is running off toward my lot in a way it didn't used to. Could we take a look at the property line together, especially behind the patio, and think through what a permanent fix might look like? With more rain likely over the coming weeks, I'd rather get ahead of it than let another storm do the talking.

2. The fill and fence along our shared line.

I've also noticed that some of the fill from your side has been migrating under the edge of your slab onto my patio, and in one stretch the boundary fence has been pushed to the ground. I know these kinds of details can get missed at the tail end of a big construction project. I'd appreciate a chance to walk the line with you and figure out a sensible fix on the retaining side of things — I don't think it needs to be a big production.

3. Exterior lights at night.

A few of the exterior fixtures on your building shine directly onto my dwelling and yard and stay on through the night. The easiest fix here is probably shielding or redirecting the fixtures — whatever makes sense on your end so the light stays primarily on your property. This one is the smallest of the items but it would make a real difference for me.

4. Something I noticed after the rain.

I want to flag one more thing so you can check it on your end. After one of the storm events I observed what looked and smelled like sanitary effluent coming from the direction of your side of the property line and flowing onto mine. I'm not claiming to know the source, and I'd appreciate if you could have your plumber have a look at your lateral and let me know what they find. If the source turns out to be somewhere else entirely, I'd rather we figure that out together than have either of us guessing.

I'm writing in the hope of resolving these privately, and I realize you may not yet have had a chance to see what each of these conditions looks like from my side. I welcome your inspection and a conversation before either of us has to involve outside parties. Could you give me a call in the next couple of weeks so we can walk the line and talk through this? Easiest way to reach me is [PHONE].

Thanks for taking a look.
