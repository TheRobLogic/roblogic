To the Code Compliance Supervisor:

Executive summary

I own the parcels at 4852 and 4854 Palm Avenue (APNs 4942510700 and 4942510600), immediately south of 4862 Palm. B21-885-REV-01 was Finaled on 1/5/2026 at the adjoining parcel. Four as-built conditions on the Salameh lots are in violation of the La Mesa Municipal Code and are causing continuing injury to my property:

  • Raised fill at the shared line with no engineered retaining wall; soil migration onto my lot; deflection of the boundary fence (§I.1).
  • Altered drainage discharging runoff across my lot (§I.2).
  • Observed discharge of what I believed to be sanitary sewage effluent onto my parcel; source not demonstrated corrected (§I.3).
  • Unshielded exterior lighting illuminating my dwelling and yard (§I.4).

I request the City (1) open code-enforcement cases under LMMC Ch. 10.150 and Ch. 10.170 for Grievances I and IV; (2) refer Grievances II and III internally to the City Engineer and externally to San Diego County DEHQ and the Regional Water Board; (3) require the owner, within 30 days, to submit an engineered remediation plan addressing items (a)–(d) in §III.5, permitted and inspected through the Building Division. A written decision with a stated timeline is part of the administrative record I am entitled to.

The permit numbers cited below are from the 19-page record produced by the City in response to my California Public Records Act request PRA #26-237 (filed 4/14/2026, closed 4/16/2026). A narrower CPRA follow-up to the City Clerk is being served separately.

I. Violations complained of

  1. Unpermitted grading and fill; loss of lateral support.
    The applicant raised finished grade on the 4862 side of our shared property line by approximately one to two feet with no engineered retaining wall at the boundary. Exact depth is to be confirmed by survey; the City's inspector should measure it at the line. Fill material is migrating from beneath the edge of the applicant's concrete slab onto my parcel. Lateral pressure has deflected the shared boundary fence — in one location to ground level. My parcel, prior to the applicant's grading, was in its previously-supported natural state; the conditions described are the direct consequence of the change next door. See Exhibits 4, 5, 6, and 9B (GPS-verified at the shared line): Exhibit 4 (2024-10-13) shows the construction state with no retaining wall at the slab edge and a fully collapsed fence section; Exhibit 5 shows the grade differential between Salameh's raised fill and my concrete patio, with a plywood panel in place of any engineered retaining wall; Exhibit 6 shows dirt migrating from beneath the slab edge onto my patio surface; Exhibit 9B (2024-09-27, Live Photo keyframe) is a low-angle cross-section showing the chain-link fence deflected to the ground under fill, with loose soil spilling onto my side. Exhibit 9 (2024-09-27) establishes that the condition pre-dated the October 2024 documentation by over two weeks.
    • The grading appears to have altered the natural drainage course (see §I.2 below), which would remove it from the "no permit" exemption of CBC Appendix J, §J103.2(8), as adopted by LMMC §14.05.020, regardless of depth or volume.
    • LMMC §24.05.030(K) requires residential grading to be "minimal" and to "leave natural appearing landforms."
    • LMMC §24.05.030(I)(6) limits any retaining wall in a required side-yard setback to the height needed to retain a three-foot fill; no such wall exists.
    • Cal. Civ. Code §832 guarantees the lateral and subjacent support my land receives from the adjoining lot in its prior natural condition. The migrating fill and fence deflection are consistent with a withdrawal of that support.
    • The shared boundary fence is subject to Cal. Civ. Code §841. See Exhibits 1, 2, 3, 7, and 8: fence damage, displaced top rail, construction lumber against the fence on the applicant's side, and fresh-cut wood with sawdust at fence-post locations. A separate §841(b) statutory notice of fence damage and cost-share will be served on the adjoining owner under separate cover. The fence condition is raised here only as evidence of the §I.1 violations and as a related public record.
  2. Altered drainage; runoff and flooding.
    The regraded 4862 lot now drains toward my property. On [DATE OF STORM], I observed runoff discharging from 4862 across the shared line onto my lot. On the same date, my basement flooded and a mature orange tree uprooted in saturated soil along the shared line; the runoff from 4862 was a substantial contributing factor to each. The City's inspector should assess the as-built drainage. Smaller runoff events have occurred on [LIST OTHER DATES IF KNOWN].
    • LMMC §7.18.040(A)–(B): the discharge of sediment and construction-related waste into the City's MS4 is an illegal discharge. §7.18.140(A): eroded soils on disturbed slopes must be secured before the rainy season. §7.18.220: "any violation of this chapter is a public nuisance." Penalties are set by §7.18.230.
    • Cal. Civ. Code §§3479–3481: private and public nuisance.
    • Keys v. Romley, 64 Cal.2d 396 (1966) (reasonable-use rule governs alterations to natural drainage between adjoining private landowners).
  3. Sewage-effluent discharge onto my property.
    On [DATE OF SEWAGE INCIDENT], I observed what I believed to be sanitary sewage effluent discharging from the 4862 side of our shared line onto my parcel. The condition has not been demonstrated to be permanently corrected. I am reporting this observation to the San Diego County Department of Environmental Health & Quality (1-888-846-0800) and to the San Diego Regional Water Quality Control Board (Region 9) via the CalEPA Environmental Complaint System. The City Engineer should inspect the 4862 sewer lateral under the authority cited below.
    • LMMC §17.08.030 authorizes the City Engineer to inspect any lateral discharging to the public sewer and to issue a corrective directive where improper use or maintenance of a lateral "causes or contributes to the discharge of septic wastewater… or any other objectionable substance."
    • LMMC §7.18.040(B)(1) identifies sewage as a prohibited non-stormwater discharge.
    • Cal. Health & Safety Code §5411 and Cal. Water Code §13304(a) are the statutory predicates under which DEHQ and the Regional Water Board have independent jurisdiction to inspect, abate, and order cleanup. I do not assert a private right of action under either statute.
  4. Light trespass from unshielded fixtures.
    Multiple unshielded exterior lights installed on the 4862 structure cast illumination directly onto my dwelling and yard. I have observed these fixtures operating through overnight hours on [DATES OBSERVED].
    • LMMC §24.05.020(D)(16): "All lighting shall be designed, installed and maintained to project the light primarily on the owner's property… Any light falling on adjacent properties shall be minimal and incidental. Lighting shall be focused directly on the owner's property, and shall not be focused on adjacent properties."
    • Cal. Civ. Code §3479 (nuisance — anything offensive to the senses that interferes with the comfortable enjoyment of property).

II. Related permit-record observations

Two items in the PRA #26-237 production bear directly on the scope of the violations above:

  • Apparent situs mismatch on B21-885-REV-01. The revision permit for the current structure on 4862 shows "Finaled" on 1/5/2026 at situs "4860 Palm Ave." No separate 4860 Palm Ave parcel is reflected in the Assessor record (parcels adjoining are 4862, 4870, 4852, 4854). The Building Division should confirm the address of record on that final and confirm that the as-built improvements match the approved scope of B21-885.
  • Scope of PORT2024-0816 (VOIDED). The plan-change application for 4862 — which the City's public portal describes as including second- and third-floor balconies, balcony roof covers, new stairs and footings, new entry doors, and a trash enclosure — is shown as VOIDED on the portal. If any of the work described in that voided application was nonetheless performed as part of the completed construction, it is unpermitted. The Building Division should confirm by site inspection which elements of that scope, if any, were built.

III. Requested actions

Requested actions:

  1. Open a code-enforcement case for Grievances I and IV. Refer Grievance II internally to the City Engineer and Public Works for drainage review. Refer Grievance III internally to the City Engineer under LMMC §17.08.030. Grievance III also lies within the primary jurisdiction of San Diego County DEHQ and the Regional Water Board and has been reported there in parallel.
  2. Compare the as-built structure and site work at 4862 against the approved scope of B21-885 (and REV-01) by site inspection. Determine whether any work described in voided permit PORT2024-0816 was in fact performed during the now-completed construction.
  3. Review the B21-885-REV-01 "Finaled" determination dated 1/5/2026 in light of the site conditions documented in Exhibits 1–9B and the situs discrepancy in §II. If any Certificate of Occupancy was issued, suspend that certificate pending full abatement of the §I conditions under LMMC Ch. 10.170.
  4. Issue a Notice of Violation and initiate administrative proceedings under LMMC Ch. 10.150 (nuisance abatement) and Ch. 10.170 (land-use violations), with the authority to assess civil penalties, record a cost-recovery lien against the property, and impose joint and several personal liability on the owner under §10.170.030(b)(5)(a). Each day the §I conditions persist is a continuing violation under Ch. 10.150, Ch. 10.170, and Ch. 7.18.
  5. Require the owner, within 30 days of any NOV, to submit an engineered remediation plan addressing each of the following, permitted and inspected through the Building Division and signed off by Public Works where applicable: (a) an engineered retaining wall at the shared property line sufficient to contain the existing fill, per LMMC §24.05.030(I)(6) and CBC §105.2 exception 3; (b) site drainage returning pre-development runoff conditions or better; (c) sewer-lateral inspection, pressure-test, and certification per LMMC §17.08.030, with documentation of any pump-related remediation; (d) replacement of non-conforming exterior lighting with shielded, downward-directed fixtures compliant with §24.05.020(D)(16).
  6. Condition any future permit, plan revision, or further occupancy sign-off on these parcels on full completion of the above remediation. Issue a written decision on this intake — including the specific code-enforcement disposition of each grievance and any referral made — within 30 days of receipt, so that the administrative record is complete and reviewable (CCP §§1085, 1094.5).

V. Reservation of rights

This letter is written pursuant to my rights as an aggrieved adjoining property owner and is without prejudice to any civil remedies available to me under Cal. Civ. Code §§832, 841, 3479–3481, including abatement, damages, and injunctive relief. Each of the §I conditions is a continuing nuisance / continuing trespass under Baker v. Burbank-Glendale-Pasadena Airport Auth., 39 Cal.3d 862 (1985): each day the conditions persist is a new accrual of injury for purposes of the statute of limitations (CCP §338(b)) and the election of abatement versus damages.

A separate Civil Code §841(b)(2) statutory notice concerning the shared boundary fence — containing the statutorily-enumerated content (nature of the problem, proposed solution, estimated cost, proposed cost allocation, and timeline) and providing the required 30-day response window — will be served on the adjoining owner under separate cover. Nothing in this letter constitutes that §841 notice, nor a waiver of it, nor an election of remedies as between the administrative remedies requested herein and the civil remedies reserved.

A citizen complaint to a public agency is not a settlement communication within the meaning of Cal. Evid. Code §1152. Each factual assertion in this letter is within my personal observation or taken from the City's own permit record, and I am available to verify any item under oath if required.
