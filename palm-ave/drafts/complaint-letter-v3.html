I ask the City to (1) open code-enforcement cases under LMMC Ch. 10.150 and Ch. 10.170 for Grievances I and IV; (2) refer Grievances II and III internally to the City Engineer and externally to San Diego County DEHQ and the Regional Water Board; and (3) require the owner to submit, within 30 days, an engineered remediation plan addressing items (a)–(d) in §III.5, to be permitted and inspected through the Building Division. A written decision with a stated timeline is requested.

The construction at 4862 is now complete: the City's public permit portal shows B21-885-REV-01 "Finaled" on 1/5/2026. I deferred submitting a formal complaint until that final was recorded, because only then did the as-built conditions described below become the permanent state of the property and their ongoing effect on mine ripen from a construction-phase irritation into a continuing injury. Read with that framing, each of the conditions I describe is a continuing violation / continuing trespass under California law (Baker v. Burbank-Glendale-Pasadena Airport Auth., 39 Cal.3d 862 (1985)) that re-accrues each day it persists.

This letter should be read alongside the 19-page permit-history record produced by the City in response to my California Public Records Act request PRA #26-237 (filed 4/14/2026, closed 4/16/2026), which I rely on for the permit numbers cited below. A further, narrower CPRA request addressed to the City Clerk follows separately.

I. Violations complained of

  1. Unpermitted grading and fill; loss of lateral support.
    The applicant has raised finished grade on the 4862 side of our shared property line by approximately one to two feet (exact depth to be confirmed by survey; I request the City's inspector measure it at the line) with no engineered retaining wall at the boundary. Fill material is migrating from beneath the edge of the applicant's concrete slab onto my parcel, and lateral pressure has deflected the shared boundary fence — in one location to ground level. My parcel, prior to the applicant's grading, was in its previously-supported natural state; the conditions I describe are the direct consequence of the change next door. See Exhibits 4, 5, 6, and 9B (GPS-verified at the shared line): Exhibit 4 (2024-10-13) shows the construction state with no retaining wall at the slab edge and a fully collapsed fence section; Exhibit 5 shows the grade differential between Salameh's raised fill and my concrete patio, with a plywood panel in place of any engineered retaining wall; Exhibit 6 shows dirt migrating from beneath the slab edge onto my patio surface; and Exhibit 9B (2024-09-27, Live Photo keyframe) is a low-angle cross-section showing the chain-link fence deflected to the ground under fill, with loose soil spilling onto my side. Exhibit 9 (2024-09-27) establishes that the condition pre-dated the October 2024 documentation by over two weeks.
    • The grading has altered the natural drainage course (see §I.2 below), which removes it from the "no permit" exemption of CBC Appendix J, §J103.2(8), as adopted by LMMC §14.05.020, regardless of depth or volume.
    • LMMC §24.05.030(K) requires residential grading to be "minimal" and to "leave natural appearing landforms."
    • LMMC §24.05.030(I)(6) limits any retaining wall in a required side-yard setback to the height needed to retain a three-foot fill; no such wall exists.
    • Cal. Civ. Code §832 guarantees the lateral and subjacent support my land receives from the adjoining lot in its prior natural condition. The migrating fill and fence deflection are consistent with a withdrawal of that support.
    • The shared boundary fence is subject to Cal. Civ. Code §841. See Exhibits 1, 2, 3, 7, and 8: fence damage, displaced top rail, construction lumber against the fence on the applicant's side, and fresh-cut wood with sawdust at fence-post locations. A separate §841(b) statutory notice of fence damage and cost-share will be served on the adjoining owner under separate cover; I raise the fence condition here only as evidence of the §I.1 violations and as a related public record.
  2. Altered drainage; runoff and flooding.
    The regraded 4862 lot now drains toward my property. On [DATE OF STORM], I observed runoff discharging from 4862 across the shared line onto my lot. On the same date, my basement flooded and a mature orange tree uprooted in saturated soil along the shared line; I have reason to believe the runoff from 4862 was a substantial contributing factor to each, and I request the City's inspector assess the as-built drainage. Smaller runoff events have occurred on [LIST OTHER DATES IF KNOWN].
    • LMMC §7.18.040(A)–(B): the discharge of sediment and construction-related waste into the City's MS4 is an illegal discharge. §7.18.140(A): eroded soils on disturbed slopes must be secured before the rainy season. §7.18.220: "any violation of this chapter is a public nuisance." Penalties are set by §7.18.230.
    • Cal. Civ. Code §§3479–3481: private and public nuisance.
    • Keys v. Romley, 64 Cal.2d 396 (1966) (reasonable-use rule governs alterations to natural drainage between adjoining private landowners).
  3. Sewage-effluent discharge onto my property.
    On [DATE OF SEWAGE INCIDENT], I observed what I believed to be sanitary sewage effluent discharging from the 4862 side of our shared line onto my parcel. The condition has not been demonstrated to be permanently corrected. I am contemporaneously reporting this observation to the San Diego County Department of Environmental Health & Quality (1-888-846-0800) and to the San Diego Regional Water Quality Control Board (Region 9) via the CalEPA Environmental Complaint System, and I respectfully request that the City Engineer conduct an inspection of the 4862 sewer lateral under the authority cited below.
    • LMMC §17.08.030 authorizes the City Engineer to inspect any lateral discharging to the public sewer and to issue a corrective directive where the improper use or maintenance of a lateral "causes or contributes to the discharge of septic wastewater… or any other objectionable substance."
    • LMMC §7.18.040(B)(1) identifies sewage as a prohibited non-stormwater discharge.
    • Cal. Health & Safety Code §5411 and Cal. Water Code §13304(a) are cited here as the statutory predicates under which DEHQ and the Regional Water Board have independent jurisdiction to inspect, abate, and order cleanup; I do not assert a private right of action under either statute.
  4. Light trespass from unshielded fixtures.
    Multiple unshielded exterior lights installed on the 4862 structure cast illumination directly onto my dwelling and yard. I have observed these fixtures operating through overnight hours on [DATES OBSERVED].
    • LMMC §24.05.020(D)(16): "All lighting shall be designed, installed and maintained to project the light primarily on the owner's property… Any light falling on adjacent properties shall be minimal and incidental. Lighting shall be focused directly on the owner's property, and shall not be focused on adjacent properties."
    • Cal. Civ. Code §3479 (nuisance — anything offensive to the senses that interferes with the comfortable enjoyment of property).

V. Reservation of rights

This letter is written pursuant to my rights as an aggrieved adjoining property owner and is without prejudice to any civil remedies available to me under Cal. Civ. Code §§832, 841, 3479–3481, and §§3501–3503, including abatement, damages, and injunctive relief. I expressly plead the continuing nuisance / continuing trespass doctrine (Baker v. Burbank-Glendale-Pasadena Airport Auth., 39 Cal.3d 862 (1985)): each day that the §I conditions persist is a new accrual of injury for purposes of the statute of limitations (CCP §338(b)) and the election of abatement versus damages.

A separate Civil Code §841(b)(2) statutory notice concerning the shared boundary fence — containing the statutorily-enumerated content (nature of the problem, proposed solution, estimated cost, proposed cost allocation, and timeline) and providing the required 30-day response window — will be served on the adjoining owner under separate cover. Nothing in this letter should be construed as that §841 notice, nor as a waiver of it, nor as an election of remedies as between the administrative remedies requested herein and the civil remedies reserved.

I acknowledge that a citizen complaint to a public agency is not a settlement communication within the meaning of Cal. Evid. Code §1152, and I have drafted accordingly; each factual assertion in this letter is within my personal observation or taken from the City's own permit record, and I am available to verify any item under oath if required.
