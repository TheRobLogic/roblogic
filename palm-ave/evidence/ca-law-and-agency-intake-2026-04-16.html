CA Statutes, Case Law, & San Diego Agency Intake

Compiled 2026-04-16 for Palm Ave dispute (Vikunja #508). Public records only. Not legal advice.

Statutes

Cal. Civil Code § 832 — Lateral and subjacent support

"Each coterminous owner is entitled to the lateral and subjacent support which his land receives from the adjoining land, subject to the right of the owner of the adjoining land to make proper and usual excavations on the same for purposes of construction or improvement…"

leginfo.legislature.ca.gov — Civ. Code §832

Cal. Civil Code § 841 — Division fences

"(a) Adjoining landowners shall share equally in the responsibility for maintaining the boundaries and monuments between them. (b)(1) Adjoining landowners are presumed to share an equal benefit from any fence dividing their properties and, unless otherwise agreed to by the parties in a written agreement, shall be presumed to be equally responsible for the reasonable costs of construction, maintenance, or necessary replacement of the fence."

leginfo.legislature.ca.gov — Civ. Code §841

Cal. Civil Code § 3479 — Nuisance (definition)

"Anything which is injurious to health… or is indecent or offensive to the senses, or an obstruction to the free use of property, so as to interfere with the comfortable enjoyment of life or property… is a nuisance."

leginfo.legislature.ca.gov — Civ. Code §3479

Cal. Civil Code § 3480 — Public nuisance

"A public nuisance is one which affects at the same time an entire community or neighborhood, or any considerable number of persons, although the extent of the annoyance or damage inflicted upon individuals may be unequal."

leginfo.legislature.ca.gov — Civ. Code §3480

Cal. Civil Code § 3481 — Private nuisance

"Every nuisance not included in the definition of the last section is private."

leginfo.legislature.ca.gov — Civ. Code §3481

Cal. Civil Code §§ 3501–3503 — Abatement

§3501: "The remedies against a private nuisance are: 1. A civil action; or, 2. Abatement." (§§3502-3503 authorize self-help abatement by the injured party but limit force and require minimal damage.)

leginfo — Civ. Code §§3501–3503 — Flag: §§3502/3503 not individually verified via direct fetch; confirm exact wording before citing.

Cal. Water Code § 13304(a) — Cleanup orders

"A person who has discharged or discharges waste into the waters of this state in violation of any waste discharge requirement or other order or prohibition… shall, upon order of the regional board, clean up the waste or abate the effects of the waste."

leginfo — Water Code §13304

Cal. Health & Safety Code § 5411 — Sewage discharge prohibition

"No person shall discharge sewage or other waste, or the effluent of treated sewage or other waste, in any manner which will result in contamination, pollution or a nuisance." (§§5410, 5412-5416 supply definitions, reporting duties, and penalties.)

leginfo — H&S Code §5411 — Flag: §§5410/5412-5416 not individually verified; confirm before citing.

Cal. Gov. Code § 7922.530 — CPRA request mechanism

"(a) Except with respect to public records exempt from disclosure by express provisions of law, each state or local agency, upon a request for a copy of records that reasonably describes an identifiable record or records, shall make the records promptly available to any person upon payment of fees covering direct costs of duplication, or a statutory fee if applicable. Upon request, an exact copy shall be provided unless impracticable to do so."

leginfo — Gov. Code §7922.530

Case Law

Keys v. Romley, 64 Cal.2d 396 (1966)

California Supreme Court adopted the "reasonable use" rule for surface waters, rejecting both common-enemy and civil-law doctrines.

"It is incumbent upon every person to take reasonable care in using his property to avoid injury to adjacent property through the flow of surface waters."

Liability turns on reasonableness — measured by amount of harm, foreseeability, actor's purpose, and whether utility of the use outweighs gravity of harm. Factual inquiry in every case.

Justia — Keys v. Romley

Locklin v. City of Lafayette, 7 Cal.4th 327 (1994)

Confirmed the reasonable-use rule applies even where conduct alters a natural watercourse, and extended it to public entities.

"Past decisions do not provide immunity under the natural watercourse rule for conduct which alters natural drainage and thereby creates a danger to downstream property owners if that danger is unreasonable in light of the purpose of the upstream action, the manner in which it is carried out, and the alternatives that might avoid or mitigate the potential damage."

Justia — Locklin v. City of Lafayette

Sheffet v. County of Los Angeles, 3 Cal.App.3d 720 (1970)

Court of Appeal applied Keys to upper-landowner liability for altered drainage — directly on point for a regraded, fill-dirt lot sending runoff downhill:

"No party, whether an upper or a lower landowner, may act arbitrarily and unreasonably in his relations with other landowners and still be immunized from all liability."

Justia — Sheffet v. L.A.

Priority Filing Path

Three parallel filings maximize leverage:

  1. La Mesa Code Compliance (SeeClickFix + paper form) — unpermitted grading/fill + fence damage under Civ Code §841. Fastest physical inspection; can issue stop-work & abatement orders. This is the local pressure lever.
  2. Regional Water Board Region 9 via CalEPA portal — stormwater + sewage discharge under Water Code §13304 and H&S §5411. Regulatory teeth the city lacks; §13304 cleanup orders enforceable by AG injunction. Produces auditable paper trail.
  3. SD County DEHQ 24-hour hotline 1-888-846-0800 — for sewage overflow specifically. Immediate-response channel; sewage contamination triggers reporting duties and creates contemporaneous agency documentation that buttresses a later private-nuisance action under Civ. Code §§3479/3481.

Together: (a) on-site response (DEHQ), (b) structural enforcement with statutory cleanup authority (Region 9), (c) local stop-work/permit leverage (La Mesa).

Note on light trespass: weak at state level. Raise under La Mesa nuisance ordinance AND as a Civ Code §3479 "offensive to the senses" element of the broader nuisance claim. Don't file a standalone state complaint for lighting.
