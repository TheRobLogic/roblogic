La Mesa Municipal Code — Citations for 4862 Palm Complaint

Compiled 2026-04-16. All text quoted verbatim from Municode Library (current version March 31, 2026). 4862 Palm is zoned R-3 (Multiple Unit Residential).

1. Impervious Surface / Lot Coverage (R-3)

LMMC §24.05.030(B) & fn.4

"Coverage. All buildings, including accessory buildings and structures shall cover not more than forty percent of the lot area." (fn. 4 to Table of Dimensions)

R-3 minimums: lot area 14,000 sf · width 70' · depth 70' · front setback 15' · side setback 5' per story plus 4' for walls >100' long · height 30'.

Applies here: The 40% cap covers buildings and structures. LMMC does not impose a separate impervious-surface (paving) percentage cap.

Municode — Title 24 Ch 24.05

Ambiguity: The main §24.05.030(B) table shows a dash in the R-3 "Coverage" row. Footnote 4 applies the 40% cap universally. A planner could theoretically argue the dash = no cap for R-3. Cite both footnote 4 AND the table; let Code Compliance resolve.

2. Grading & Fill Dirt

LMMC §14.05.010 & §14.05.020 (adopts CBC Appendix J, amended 2022)

§14.05.010: "There is hereby adopted by the City of La Mesa… Appendix J of the California Building Code, 2022 Edition..."

§14.05.020 (J103.2 Exemptions): "A grading permit shall not be required for the following: 8. An excavation or fill which is less than three (3) feet (915 mm) in depth and does not create a slope steeper than two horizontal to one vertical and does not exceed 100 cubic yards (76.5 m³) on any one lot and does not obstruct or alter a drainage course."

Also — §24.05.030(B) fn. *3c: "If a building site is filled to more than three (3) feet above natural grade, the maximum height for a building within 30 feet of property line shall be reduced by the difference between the natural grade and 'grade'..."

§24.05.030(K): "Any grading done in connection with development of residential properties shall be minimal and the resulting development will leave natural appearing landforms."

Applies here: Salameh's ~2 ft fill and altered drainage onto Rob's lot → permit required (grading alters drainage course, which removes the exemption regardless of depth/volume). And if fill exceeds 3 ft, the height reduction rule in §24.05.030(B) fn. *3c kicks in. CBC Appendix J also mandates compliance with §J108 (protection of adjoining property) and §J109 (drainage).

3. Retaining Walls

LMMC §24.05.030(I)(6)

"Retaining walls within any required minimum front, rear or side yard setback shall not exceed a height needed to retain (i) a fill of three feet or (ii) a cut of six feet."

§24.05.030(I) prefatory: fence/wall height "measured from the midpoint between the finished surface grades on both sides of the fence or wall, measured along sections not exceeding ten feet in length."

Applies here: If Salameh's fill exceeds 3 ft within the side-yard setback, he needs both (a) a special permit for the retaining wall and (b) the retaining wall itself, which he hasn't built. Also, CBC §105.2 exception 3 requires a building permit for any retaining wall >4 ft from footing bottom to wall top.

4. Stormwater Management / Runoff

LMMC Ch 7.18 — Storm Water Management and Discharge Control Ordinance (Ord. 2015-2840)

§7.18.040(A) — Illegal Discharge

"Any discharge that is not in compliance with this chapter is an illegal discharge. The City maintains legal authority to control the contributions of pollutants in discharges of runoff into the City's MS4 associated with industrial, commercial, construction, and residential activities."

§7.18.040(B) — Prohibited Non-Storm-Water

"No person shall discharge non-storm water directly or indirectly into the storm water conveyance system or receiving waters. Prohibited are illegal discharges or illegal connections that include but are not limited to: 1. Sewage; … 5. Discharge of wash water from the cleaning or hosing of impervious surfaces… 9. Discharges of sediment, pet waste, vegetation clippings, or other landscape or construction related wastes…"

§7.18.140(A) — Eroded Soils

"Prior to the rainy season, dischargers must remove or secure any significant accumulations of eroded soils from slopes previously disturbed by clearing or grading, if those eroded soils could otherwise enter the storm water conveyance system or receiving waters during the rainy season. Slopes more than five feet in height, more than two hundred fifty square feet in total area, and steeper than 3:1… shall be continuously protected from erosion."

§7.18.220 — Public Nuisance

"The City council hereby declares that any violation of this chapter is a public nuisance."

§7.18.230 — Penalties

Misdemeanor with fines up to $1,000 and/or 6 months; civil penalties; abatement; liens; injunctive relief.
Applies here: Sediment and sewage-mixed runoff reaching the MS4 / public storm drain = illegal discharge AND city-declared public nuisance. Unsecured graded slope near the property line triggers §7.18.140(A) if it meets the height/area/slope threshold.
Gap: Ch 7.18 only bites when runoff reaches the MS4/storm drain or receiving waters. Pure lot-to-lot drainage where nothing reaches a public drain is governed by common-law private nuisance (Cal. Civ. Code §§3479-3480) and the CA "reasonable use" surface-water rule (Keys v. Romley) — not LMMC.

5. Sewage & Sewer Laterals

LMMC §17.08.030

"The city engineer may inspect any lateral or collecting sewer system that discharges wastewater directly or indirectly to the public sewer. If the city engineer determines that the improper use, maintenance or construction of a lateral… causes or contributes to the discharge of septic wastewater, excessive groundwater, debris or any other objectionable substance to the public sewer, the city engineer may give notice of the unsatisfactory condition to any discharger contributing to such condition and shall direct that condition be corrected. In the event of a failure to comply with the city engineer's directive, the city may disconnect such lateral…"

§17.08.010: City builds laterals to the property line; everything from the building to the property line is private and owner-maintained.

§7.18.040(B)(1): Sewage is a prohibited illegal discharge to stormwater.

Applies here: Salameh's failed sewage pump → overflow onto Rob's lot → "objectionable substance" under §17.08.030 AND illegal discharge under §7.18.040(B)(1). City Engineer can order abatement.

Sewage overflow reporting: La Mesa Public Works 619-667-1450 (business hours); Police non-emergency 619-667-1400 (after hours, 24/7).

6. Outdoor Lighting / Light Trespass

LMMC §24.05.020(D)(16)

"Exterior lighting shall be compatible with residential use. All lighting shall be designed, installed and maintained to project the light primarily on the owner's property. This may require the use of shields, and may limit the location, type and height of light fixtures. Any light falling on adjacent properties shall be minimal and incidental. Lighting shall be focused directly on the owner's property, and shall not be focused on adjacent properties."
Applies here: This is the on-point light-trespass ordinance. Unshielded security lights casting direct glare onto Rob's lot violate §24.05.020(D)(16) verbatim. La Mesa doesn't have a separate dark-sky, foot-candle, or operating-hours ordinance — this section IS the enforceable standard.

7. Nuisance & Code Enforcement Procedure

§10.150.020 — Nuisance Defined

"The city council declares that any condition caused, maintained or permitted to exist in violation of any provision of this code which obstructs, injures, or interferes with the reasonable or free use of property… shall be deemed a public nuisance and may be summarily abated…"

§10.170.020 — Land Use Violations

"Whenever the director has reason to believe that a use or condition exists in violation of any land use ordinance… he or she is authorized to initiate enforcement under Chapter 10.180, abatement pursuant to Chapter 10.150, and/or may commence an administrative proceeding under this chapter to cause the assessment of a civil penalty…"

§10.170.030(b)(5)(a) — Liens & Personal Liability

"If any required work is not commenced or completed within the times specified, the director will proceed to cause abatement of the violation and cause the work to be done and charge the costs thereof as a lien against the property and as a joint and separate personal obligation of any person in violation…"
Applies here: The city has three overlapping tools: (1) summary public-nuisance abatement with recorded notice, liens, cost recovery; (2) administrative enforcement of zoning/building/land-use violations with civil penalties; (3) criminal misdemeanor/infraction charges (e.g., §7.18.230). Lien + personal liability on Salameh individually is the strongest leverage.

Gaps & Honest Notes

  • R-3 lot coverage text internally inconsistent (table dash vs. footnote 4). Cite both.
  • No general impervious-surface % cap in LMMC.
  • No lot-to-lot drainage statute — rely on common-law nuisance (Keys v. Romley).
  • No retaining-wall setback from property line — only height limits within setbacks. CBC Appendix J supplies the rest.
  • Sewer-lateral maintenance responsibility set by City policy, not a direct LMMC line.
  • No dark-sky / hours / foot-candle lighting standard — §24.05.020(D)(16) is the only hook.
