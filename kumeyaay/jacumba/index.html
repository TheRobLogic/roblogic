Jacumba Hot Springs

where the brothers emerged from the ground

A geothermal spring at the eastern descent of the Peninsular Ranges, on the international border. Emergence site of the Kumeyaay creator brothers.

The Place

Jacumba sits at the eastern descent of the Peninsular Ranges, at 2,829 ft elevation, on the international border. The Mexican-side village of Jacumé sits directly opposite. Historically the two were a single Kumeyaay community on the trans-peninsular trail; an unmanned border crossing connected them until 1995.

The Springs

Sulfur-bearing geothermal springs flow at approximately 104° F (40° C). Kumeyaay singers bathed at the springs using native grasses and hot mud for spiritual purification, a practice publicly documented in Viejas Band tradition.1

The Emergence Tradition

Per Viejas Band published tradition — citing Constance Goddard DuBois's 1907 ethnographic record — the creator brothers Tuchaipa and Yokomatis emerged from the ground at the base of a hill at the springs.2

The Kachawharr travel song — a Jacumba dance-song accompanied by a basket rubbed with a stick — originated here. It was taught first to the Manzanita people, then spread to other Kumeyaay settlements. The Kachawharr cycle includes narratives of the brothers hunting cottontail rabbits and competing in games.

Deep Occupation

ASM Affiliates archaeological work has dated Kumeyaay-ancestor occupation in the Jacumba area to approximately 10,000 years. The 544-square-kilometer Jacumba/McCain Valley study area contains 144 documented prehistoric sites and 11 possible Traditional Cultural Properties.3

The Bureau of Land Management has determined the Jacumba Discontiguous Archaeological District (JDAD) eligible for the National Register under Criterion D (archaeology). The La Rumorosa pictograph style — red, black, and white anthropomorphs and geometric figures — centers at El Vallecito just south in Baja California, with panels extending into the Jacumba corridor.

Modern Site

The original Hotel Jacumba opened circa 1925 and peaked in the 1930s as a spa retreat. I-8 bypassed the town in the 1960s. The hotel burned in a 1983 arson fire. New ownership purchased the property in October 2020; the renovated Jacumba Hot Springs Hotel held its grand reopening in April 2024.

Wall Construction

Kumeyaay burial sites near Jacumba were named in the 2020 La Posta Band v. Trump federal lawsuit over first-term border-wall construction.4

In late April 2025, DHS Secretary Kristi Noem signed an environmental-laws waiver — the first of the second Trump administration — clearing approximately 2.5 miles of new wall construction at Jacumba Hot Springs (plus Smuggler's Gulch and Otay Mesa). This work is geographically separate from the Cuchumá blasting roughly 15 miles west but operates under the same NSPM-4 framework and December 2025 Navy land transfer.

The youth-led cross-tribal group Kumeyaay Defense Against the Wall has staged ongoing civil disobedience at the Jacumba wall site, including a notable BLM eviction action in September 2020.
