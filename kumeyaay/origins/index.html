II · Cosmology

Origins

where the songs point

The regional Yuman emergence at Spirit Mountain, the Kumeyaay creation cycle of twin brothers, and the sacred geography that frames everything else.

Avi Kwa Ame

The dominant regional Yuman emergence point is Avi Kwa Ame / Wikami — Spirit Mountain, on the present Nevada–Arizona border south of Laughlin. The mountain was designated a U.S. national monument in 2023 following sustained advocacy by Yuman peoples.

From Avi Kwa Ame, Yuman-speaking peoples are said to have dispersed: the Quechan southward to Yuma, the Cocopa to the Colorado delta, and ancestral Kumeyaay westward into the Peninsular Ranges and to the Pacific coast.

Tuchaipa & Yokomatis

Kumeyaay creation tradition centers on twin brothers Tuchaipa (elder, also Tcaipakomat or Maayhaay) and Yokomatis.

The world was originally covered in salt water. The brothers raised the land, shaped the people, and instituted the order of life. The Viejas Band publicly identifies Jacumba Hot Springs as the place where the brothers emerged from the ground at the base of a hill at the spring.1

Tuchaipa was poisoned by Frog and died. His death instituted the mourning ceremony — the Keruk/Takaay still performed today — and his spirit became the moon, which is why the moon wanes and is reborn each cycle.

Maayhaay

The Creator-spirit Maayhaay set the sacred geography of the Kumeyaay world. Maayhaay placed Cuchumá as the central locus of power for healing and peace. The shaman-spirit Kuuchamaa — sharing the mountain's name — taught the regional peoples to stop fighting one another and called the kuseyaay from across the broader Yuman world for ceremonial instruction.

Other Sacred Places

Beyond Avi Kwa Ame, Jacumba, and Cuchumá, the publicly identified sacred geography of the Kumeyaay world includes:

Ewiiy HellyaaMount Woodson
Where the moon sleeps. Bears the footprint of the spirit-giant Toyapai.
Viejas Mountainand Cowles Mountain
Solstice observatories with associated dance grounds (kwut-ahlyeah).
Yuha GeoglyphsImperial County, near Ocotillo
Ground figures made by clearing dark desert pavement to expose lighter soil. Function interpreted as ceremonial.
KuuchamaTecate Peak (variant spelling)
Same mountain as Cuchumá; older orthographic transcription.

What this page does not contain

The full cycle of clan-origin narratives, the named ceremonial protocols for the Keruk/mourning rite, and certain restricted spring and pictograph locations are intentionally held by initiated culture-bearers. What appears above is what Viejas Band, Sycuan, and ethnographic sources have made public.
