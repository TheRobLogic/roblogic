The Inventory
You Can Talk To.

A self-hosted gear tracker for a working key grip. It listens. It remembers. It pings you when Marco still has the dolly.

01 — The Idea

One walkthrough. Then conversation.

Take inventory once, the slow way — walk the truck, the kit, the cage, talking and snapping photos as you go. After that, every update is a sentence. "Marco took the C-stand kit to truck B." "Got the floppy back, scrim got bent." "Where's the 1.2k Joker?" The app keeps the ledger. You keep working.

It nudges you when something you loaned hasn't come back. It generates a wrap-out check at the end of a job. It learns your slang — your Cardellini, your stinger, your favorite ratty C47 box. Nobody else's data goes into it, and nobody else's eyes go on it. It runs on a small box at your house and reaches your phone through Tailscale, no public URL, no sign-in screen for the world to see.

03 — The Path

How it actually gets built.

Each step is an evening or two. None of it requires you to write code from scratch — you'll be prompting an AI to write it, then reading what it makes, then asking it to fix what's wrong.

  1. Stand up the workshop.

    Install Cursor (or Claude Code). Make a GitHub account if you don't have one. Create the public repo. Get a Claude API key. About an hour.

  2. Scaffold the skeleton.

    Tell the AI: FastAPI app, SQLite DB, items + locations + people tables, single HTML page that lists items. Get it running on your laptop. See your first row land.

  3. Teach it your vocabulary.

    Seed the DB with your actual kit. Add the aliases — stinger, Cardellini, 4-by. This is the one slow part. Do it once, never again.

  4. Wire the brain.

    Add a single endpoint that takes a sentence and asks Claude what to do with it. Tool-calls back into your database. Now you can talk to it.

  5. Add the nudge.

    A small background job checks for loans older than N days, fires a push to ntfy. The HelperMonkey trick, but for your gear.

  6. Move it home.

    Copy the project onto the always-on box at home. Set up Tailscale on your phone and that box. Now it reaches you on set — through your own private network, not the public internet.

  7. Live with it.

    Use it for a job. Notice what it can't do. Tell the AI to fix it. Repeat. After three jobs you'll have a tool nobody else has.

05 — What You Pick Up

The other thing you're building.

The app is one outcome. The skills are the other. By the time this thing is running, you'll know how to do the following — and that knowledge transfers to anything else you ever want to build.

  • The terminal. Enough to feel at home in it. Not a wizard, but unafraid.
  • Git. Saving versions of your work and being able to roll back when you break something.
  • Prompting an AI to write code. The real skill of 2026 — describing what you want clearly enough that the machine can make it.
  • Reading code. Not writing from scratch, but knowing what a function does and where to change one number.
  • How a web app works. Frontend, backend, database, API. The four pieces. Once you see them you see them everywhere.
  • Self-hosting. Running a thing at home and reaching it from the outside. The internet stops feeling like other people's computers.
