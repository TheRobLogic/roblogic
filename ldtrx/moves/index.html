— THE MOVES —

LOWER BODY

TRX Single-leg RDL

Single-leg hip hinge with the TRX straps as light balance assist. Trains glute, hamstring, posterior chain — and frontal-plane hip stability the bilateral version misses.

Demo: Heather Oliva Fit

Form
  • Hands light on straps for balance, not load
  • Hips square — don't rotate as the leg swings back
  • Drive through the heel, hinge from the hip not the spine
Avoid

Pulling on the straps to muscle through. The strap is a balance scaffold, not a row handle.

Bulgarian Split Squat (TRX)

Single-leg squat with the rear foot suspended in a TRX foot cradle. Hammers quads and glutes of the front leg; rear foot stays a balance point, not a pusher.

Demo: Marc Pro

Form
  • Front foot far enough that knee tracks toe at depth
  • Torso vertical, ribs stacked over hips
  • Drop straight down — don't lunge forward
Avoid

Pushing off the rear foot to assist. The rear foot only stabilizes.

Pistol Squat (TRX-assisted)

Full-depth single-leg squat with the TRX as adjustable assist. Goal over weeks: reduce strap tension until the assist disappears.

Demo: Third Space London

Form
  • Use just enough strap tension to hit full ROM
  • Working heel stays planted through the rep
  • Free leg long, ankle dorsiflexed
Avoid

Cranking reps with heavy strap pull. Less assist, more reps means progression — same assist, more reps means cheating.

Cossack Squat

Wide-stance lateral squat shifting body weight side to side. Frontal-plane hip mobility + adductor strength.

Demo: Functional Effect Fitness & Rehabilitation

Form
  • Sit deep into one hip, opposite leg extends straight
  • Keep the planted foot flat — heel doesn't lift
  • Chest up, sweep across the bottom
Avoid

Lifting the heel off the floor on the loaded side.

Skater Squat

Single-leg squat patterning where the trail leg sweeps behind the standing leg. Knee-dominant unilateral pattern that complements the hip-dominant SL-RDL.

Demo: Fitness Pain Free

Form
  • Trail knee reaches toward standing-foot heel, just off the floor
  • Slight forward lean from the hip
  • Strap or counterweight as balance only
Avoid

Touching the trail knee hard onto the floor and rebounding off it.

Step-up (TRX-assisted)

Single-leg step onto a knee-height box, TRX for balance assist. Concentric-only loading of the working leg.

Demo: Reign SportsPerformance

Form
  • Whole foot on box, knee tracks over toes
  • Drive through the working heel — no pushoff from trailing foot
  • Stand tall, lower under control
Avoid

Bouncing off the trailing foot to launch up.

Lateral Lunge (TRX-assisted)

Side-step lunge with one leg straight, the other in a deep frontal-plane squat. TRX as assist if depth is limited.

Demo: Club Sweat Fitness

Form
  • Sit back into the bent-knee hip, opposite leg extends straight
  • Keep both feet flat — full sole contact
  • Chest up, weight in the heel of the bent leg
Avoid

Letting the loaded knee cave inward as you sit down.

Shrimp Squat

Single-leg squat where the trail leg's knee lowers to the floor while the foot stays held behind. More knee-dominant than a pistol; less ankle-mobility-dependent.

Demo: The Active Life

Form
  • Hold the trail foot or use the strap as a brake
  • Touch trail knee softly to a pad
  • Drive through the front heel to stand
Avoid

Crashing the trail knee into the floor.

TRX-assisted Overhead Squat

Full-depth squat with arms held overhead in TRX straps. Forces T-spine extension and shoulder flexion through depth.

Demo: The Masters of Strength

Form
  • Arms overhead, slight tension on straps
  • Sit hips down between heels
  • Chest stays up — ribs don't dump forward
Avoid

Using strap pull to muscle the squat. Straps support form, not load.

TRX Good Morning (slow eccentric)

Bilateral hip hinge with hands in TRX straps for spinal positioning feedback. Slow eccentric loads the posterior chain.

Demo: My PT Hub

Form
  • Hands chest-height in straps, light tension
  • Push hips back, knees soft
  • Lower until you feel hamstrings, then drive hips through
Avoid

Bending from the spine instead of hinging at the hips.

TRX Hamstring Curl + Bridge

Supine hamstring curl with heels in foot cradles. Bridge held throughout — glutes locked, hamstrings curl heels toward butt.

Demo: Austin Longevity Clinic

Form
  • Heels in cradle, drive hips up first
  • Hold the bridge — don't drop hips as heels curl
  • Lower under control on the eccentric
Avoid

Letting the hips sag during the curl. The bridge is the brace.

Hip Thrust (TRX feet-in-straps)

Glute-dominant bridge with feet suspended in TRX cradles. Removes the floor's stability, forcing more glute control.

Demo: TRX Hip Thrust

Form
  • Feet shoulder-width, heels driving down through straps
  • Squeeze glutes to lock hips at top
  • Ribs stay stacked — no lumbar overarch
Avoid

Hyperextending the lower back at the top instead of finishing with glutes.

UPPER PUSH

TRX Archer Push-up

Suspended push-up where one arm straightens out as the pressing arm lowers. Closed-chain unilateral push with significant scap and serratus demand.

Demo: Integrated Strength

Form
  • Hands suspended, body locked plank-rigid
  • One arm straightens to the side as the other lowers
  • Press up explosively, switch sides
Avoid

Letting the hips sag or rotate as the offset arm extends.

TRX Push-up

Suspended push-up with hands in straps. Instability forces serratus and scap stabilizers to work hard alongside chest and triceps.

Demo: Austin Longevity Clinic

Form
  • Walk feet to set difficulty — farther from anchor = harder
  • Body straight, abs braced, no sag
  • Elbows track ~45° from torso, not flared
Avoid

Hips sagging or piking up. Lock the plank shape and don't break it.

UPPER PULL

TRX Inverted Row

Horizontal pull from a suspended position. The single most useful TRX movement — fixes the horizontal-pull deficit most CrossFit/upper-body programs leave.

Demo: Gerardi Performance

Form
  • Walk feet TOWARD the anchor for harder load (steeper body angle)
  • Pull elbows to ribs, drive shoulder blades back and down
  • Pause at top with chest near hands
Avoid

Hips sag at the bottom or pelvis pikes up. Body stays a rigid plank line.

TRX Archer Row

Unilateral horizontal pull where the off-arm extends out as the working arm rows. Anti-rotation co-stimulus on top of the pull.

Demo: Witty Wellness

Form
  • Stagger hands wide on the straps, pull one elbow to ribs
  • Working shoulder blade drives down and back
  • Off-arm stays long — don't bend it for assist
Avoid

Letting the torso twist toward the working side.

TRX Face Pull + External Rotation

High-anchor pull with elbows leading and rotation at the top. Rear delts, mid/lower traps, rotator cuff in one move.

Demo: Champion Physical Therapy and Performance

Form
  • Walk feet forward for tension, anchor is high
  • Pull hands toward temples — elbows up and out
  • Finish with pinkies up, knuckles back
Avoid

Pulling toward the chin with elbows dropped — turns it into a row.

TRX Scap Y-T-W (and I-Y-T iso)

Slow, low-load shaping work for the scapular stabilizers. Y = lower trap, T = mid trap + rear delt, W = rotator cuff + lower trap.

Demo: Duke Chiropractic

Form
  • Lean back slightly to load — short straps
  • Slow, controlled, end-range pause at each letter
  • Thumbs up throughout, ribs down
Avoid

Rushing the reps or shrugging the upper traps.

TRX Lat Hang (decompression stretch)

Hold the straps overhead, sink hips back and let lats stretch under bodyweight tension. Cool-down spinal/lat decompression.

Demo: Body Basics

Form
  • Grip straps overhead at slight angle
  • Hips sit back, arms long, breathe into ribs
  • Hold 30 seconds, two rounds
Avoid

Hunching shoulders up to ears. Let them settle long.

CORE & ANTI-ROT

TRX Pallof Press (anti-rotation)

Stand perpendicular to a split-anchor TRX, press hands out at sternum height while resisting the pull toward the anchor. Pure rotary core.

Demo: Integrated Strength

Form
  • Stand perpendicular, ribs over hips
  • Press straight out from sternum without trunk twist
  • Pause :2 at lockout — feel the rotation pull
Avoid

Letting shoulders or hips rotate toward the anchor.

Plank with Single-arm Row

Suspended plank holding both straps; one arm rows while the other holds the plank. Anti-rotation under loaded asymmetry.

Demo: Austin Longevity Clinic

Form
  • Wide feet for plank stability
  • Row elbow tight to ribs without hip rotation
  • Both straps stay stacked under the shoulders
Avoid

Hips twist toward the rowing side. Lock them.

Side Plank with Top-leg Lift

Lateral isometric — obliques, QL, glute medius all work. Top-leg lift adds glute medius load.

Demo: San Diego Chiropractic and Functional Fitness

Form
  • Stack shoulder over support elbow
  • Drive hips up — straight line head to feet
  • Lift top leg slow, no hip drop
Avoid

Hips sag toward the floor or shoulder rolls forward.

Copenhagen Plank

Side plank variant with top leg supported (often by a bench or strap). Loads adductor + obliques together — known posterior-chain protector.

Demo: [P]rehab

Form
  • Top inner-knee or ankle on bench/strap
  • Drive top leg down into the support
  • Hips up, straight line head to bottom foot
Avoid

Resting on the bottom hip instead of holding it up.

TRX Body Saw

Suspended-foot plank that rocks forward and back through the shoulders. Anti-extension under shifting lever.

Demo: CORE Strong Fitness

Form
  • Forearms down, feet in cradles
  • Rock forward and back from the shoulders, not the hips
  • Brace abs hard — body stays a plank line throughout
Avoid

Letting hips sag as you rock back.

TRX Fallout (kneeling rollout)

Kneeling, arms in straps, fall forward through the shoulders while keeping the trunk rigid. Anti-extension, pure.

Demo: COR

Form
  • Arms long, hands straight under shoulders to start
  • Reach forward — abs and lats hold the line
  • Only go as far as the lower back stays neutral
Avoid

Lower back arches as you fall forward. Stop at the limit of your brace.

TRX Pike

Suspended-foot plank to a tucked or straight-leg pike. Drives hips up while keeping legs long.

Demo: How To Perform It Correctly

Form
  • Start in a tight plank, feet in cradles
  • Lift hips up toward ceiling, legs straight (or knees in for a tuck regression)
  • Lower under control to the plank line
Avoid

Pushing hips back instead of up. The motion is vertical, not horizontal.

TRX Knee Tuck

Suspended-foot plank with knees driving in toward the chest. Lower-ab focus, also trains shoulder stability.

Demo: Strongher Programming

Form
  • Plank on hands, feet in cradles
  • Drive both knees in toward chest
  • Extend back to plank without sagging hips
Avoid

Pulling with hip flexors and forgetting to brace the trunk.

TRX Mountain Climber (slow)

Suspended-foot plank alternating knees toward chest. Slow cadence keeps it core-dominant; fast cadence turns it into metcon.

Demo: Jenny LaBaw

Form
  • Plank on hands, feet in cradles
  • Drive one knee in slow with control
  • Extend back, switch — no hip rocking
Avoid

Bouncing the hips up and down. Hips stay level.

WARM-UP & MOBILITY

TRX Deep Squat Hold + Reach

Hold a deep bodyweight squat with TRX support. Add T-spine reaches across the body to open thoracic rotation in the bottom.

Demo: Eric Cressey

Form
  • Sit deep, heels down, light strap support
  • Drive knees out, chest up
  • Reach one hand across to opposite shoulder — slow rotation
Avoid

Letting heels lift. Drop the depth or use more strap assist instead.

TRX Hip Hinge (strap-supported)

Standing hip hinge with hands lightly on straps to groove the pattern. Used in warm-up to prime the RDL pattern.

Demo: U.S. Forces Fitness

Form
  • Hands at chest height in straps, light tension
  • Push hips back, soft knees
  • Eccentric is the work — slow down on the way back
Avoid

Bending from the spine. Hinge is at the hip joint, not the lumbar.

TRX-assisted Couch Stretch

Hip-flexor + quad stretch with rear foot in TRX strap. Strap holds the foot up so you can focus on tucking the pelvis.

Demo: Vitality Wellness

Form
  • Rear foot in cradle, front foot planted in lunge
  • Tuck pelvis posteriorly — squeeze the rear glute
  • Hold :60 each side, breathe
Avoid

Hyperextending the lower back instead of tucking the pelvis.

Hip Flexor Stretch (kneeling)

Half-kneeling lunge with the back-leg hip extended. Stretches psoas + rectus femoris.

Demo: MGHOrthopaedics

Form
  • Back knee down, front foot flat
  • Tuck pelvis under — feel the stretch in the back hip, not the back
  • Slight forward shift to deepen, no lumbar arch
Avoid

Arching the lower back to fake the stretch instead of pelvic-tilting it.

90/90 Thoracic Rotation

Side-lying or seated position with knees stacked at 90/90. Open and rotate the top arm across the body, keeping hips locked.

Demo: Optimize Ottawa

Form
  • Knees stacked, top hand reaches to ceiling
  • Rotate top arm across, follow with the eyes
  • Keep both hips and knees stacked — don't let hips open
Avoid

Hip rotation cheating the t-spine motion. The hips stay locked; only the upper body moves.
