Selling the Meadowview house from San Diego

To
The heir
From
Rob Logic
Re
Your father's house in Washington County, Virginia. Who to call for every step, in order, without flying out.

If you are the only heir and there was no will, the house became yours the day he died; a $25 filing by mail proves it.1 You sign the deed in San Diego. Tax is near zero if an appraiser values the house as of the date of death before anyone touches it. Everything physical gets done by the people in section 2. The three things that can wreck it: a surviving spouse, closing inside one year of the death, and letting anyone in before the appraisal.

1.Do this, in order

  1. Answer section 3. Date of death, will or not, spouse and other children, and what the deed says decide the whole plan.
  2. Hire the attorney (call sheet A). They pull the deed, run the title search, prepare your deed, and handle the filings by mail.
  3. Locksmith rekeys the house (B) and hands the keys to the home-watch company (C), who photographs every room and outbuilding on the first visit and checks the house every two weeks after.
  4. Appraiser values the house as of the date of death (D). Nothing gets cleaned, repaired, or removed before this report exists.
  5. Insurance agent binds a vacant-dwelling policy (E). The old policy names a dead man and the house is empty; assume it will not pay.
  6. Forward the mail. Call every mortgage servicer and ask whether the loan is a reverse mortgage. Enroll in the free county recording alert (section 9). Ask the attorney to get a written Medicaid estate-recovery determination.
  7. Plumber winterizes, HVAC checks the furnace (F) before October 20, the median first freeze.
  8. Home, septic and termite inspections (G, H, I) before listing, so nothing surfaces during a buyer's inspection.
  9. Estate sale or cleanout, then photographs (J, K, L). Movers ship anything you keep (M).
  10. List it or auction it (N, O). Judge every offer on net proceeds. Close by mail-away; confirm the wire by phone.

The one date that matters most

Virginia gives his creditors one year from the death to reach the real estate. A sale by an heir inside that year is not valid against them unless the year passes quietly, which nobody can know at closing, so title insurers will not insure it.4 If the anniversary is in reach, close after it. If not, the attorney's options are to qualify you as administrator and adjudicate the debts, or negotiate an indemnity with the title underwriter.

3.What I need from you

  1. Exact date of death.
  2. Was there a will? Has anything been filed with the Washington County Circuit Court Clerk?
  3. Was he married at death? Other children, including from another marriage?
  4. What does the recorded deed say: sole name, joint with survivorship, life estate, transfer-on-death, trust? The county tax account shows one name, which proves nothing.
  5. Mortgage, HELOC, or reverse mortgage?
  6. Was he on Medicaid at 55 or older?
  7. Is homeowner's insurance in force, and when did the house actually empty?
  8. Has anyone lived in or used the house since, even rent-free? Who has keys?
  9. Was the house ever run as a business? Seven bedrooms and four and a half baths in a 1920 farmhouse reads like a boarding or care home.
  10. Vehicles, equipment, firearms, or a heating-oil tank on the property?

4.Who owns it

Check the deed first; some titling never enters the estate. Joint with survivorship and the co-owner still living: the survivor owns it, full stop. Life estate with remainder to you, or a transfer-on-death deed: yours already, no probate. "Joint tenants" with no survivorship words: a dead co-owner's half went to that person's heirs, § 55.1-134. 5.Signing from San Diego

  • Mail-away closing is the Virginia norm for out-of-state sellers. Nothing requires you at the table.
  • A California notary is valid for a Virginia deed, § 55.1-616(B).7 The one catch: California notaries cannot complete a certificate that asks them to certify your representative capacity (Cal. Civ. Code § 1189(c)); if the settlement agent's form is worded that way, use the California all-purpose acknowledgment or Virginia remote online notarization, § 47.1-13(D). Settle it in writing before signing day.
  • A Virginia attorney prepares the deed (special warranty). The Clerk e-records through Simplifile and CSC.
  • Skip the power of attorney unless you will be unreachable; underwriters reject them often. If used, property-specific, original, recent, pre-approved by the underwriter.
  • The buyer picks the settlement agent, § 55.1-1006; your attorney must consent in writing before that agent collects fees from you.

6.Taxes

  • Your basis is the value on the date of death, 26 U.S.C. § 1014, long-term automatically.20 Sold within months, net of commission, that is usually a small deductible loss ($3,000 a year against other income, rest carried forward), because a house held for sale is investment property even if nobody rented it.22 Let anyone live there rent-free and the loss disappears.
  • The county assessment ($202,300) is not fair market value in the IRS's eyes.21 Every $1,000 the date-of-death value is understated costs about $243 in federal plus California tax at the margin. The appraisal is the whole game.
  • No federal estate tax (2026 exclusion $15,000,000), no Virginia or California estate tax, and Virginia withholds nothing at closing; you sign a registration form (R-5) instead.25
  • You file federal Form 8949 and Schedule D, Virginia Form 763 with Schedule OSC, and California Form 540. Virginia gives the credit for California tax on this gain; California does not give one for Virginia's. File California first.26
  • His final 2026 federal and Virginia returns are due April 15 and May 1, 2027. If the estate sells instead of you: EIN, Form 1041, Virginia Form 770, a K-1, worse brackets, and a loss stuck inside the estate until it closes.
  • Seller-side Virginia costs: grantor's tax of $1.00 per $1,000 (§ 58.1-802), recording (calculator in section 9), and prorated county tax ($869.89 a year, halves due May 20 and November 20). Keep the appraisal, the closing statement, the 1099-S (its taxpayer number must match the deed), and every receipt.

8.Traps

  1. Closing inside one year of death. Uninsurable against his creditors, § 64.2-534(C).
  2. Reverse mortgage. Thirty days from the lender's notice to respond, foreclosure within six months of the loan coming due, and the notice goes to the empty house; payoff is capped at 95% of appraised value, 24 CFR 206.125.30
  3. Medicaid estate recovery reaches property he held any interest in at death, including non-probate interests, 12VAC30-20-141.31 Get the written determination before contracting.
  4. Foreclosure on a deed of trust needs only 14 days' notice unless the deed of trust covered an owner-occupied home, and a sale eight days after the first ad, §§ 55.1-321 and 322. Recorded heirs are who gets notice.
  5. Deed fraud. Vacant, paid-off, inherited houses are the target profile. The county's free recording alert is in section 9.
  6. Wire fraud at closing. Voice-confirm every digit at a number you looked up yourself. The FBI counted $275 million in real-estate losses in 2025.34
  7. Signing without authority: one heir signing while others hold shares, or a representative with no power of sale. Title exam and recorded List of Heirs before anything.
  8. Insurance. Virginia calls a house vacant after 30 days empty with the contents out, and lets carriers exclude freeze loss after four days unless heat stayed on or the pipes were drained, 14VAC5-341.32
  9. Cleaning out before the appraisal. The basis evidence is gone for good.
  10. Anyone occupying with verbal permission. Forfeits the 14-day emergency hearing, § 8.01-126(B). Nobody gets a key without a receipt.
  11. Releasing the deed before funds land. Virginia allows recording before funds arrive with consent, § 55.1-1008(D); instruct in writing that the deed is held until cleared funds are in.
  12. Identity theft of the deceased. Notify Social Security and the three bureaus; have the home-watch company pull documents and mail out of the house.
