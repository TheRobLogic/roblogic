Re
Your father's house in Meadowview, Washington County, Virginia: who owns it, how to sell it without flying out, how to keep the most of the money, and who to trust

You can sell this house from California. If you are the only heir and there was no will, Virginia says the house already became yours the day your father died, and the paperwork that proves it costs $25 to record.1 You can sign the deed in front of a San Diego notary.7 The tax bill should be close to zero if you get one document early: an appraisal of what the house was worth on the day he died.20 The three things that can wreck this are a surviving spouse, selling inside one year of the death, and the wrong first phone call. Every claim here cites the Virginia Code or a federal source; nothing here is legal or tax advice, and a Virginia attorney should confirm it against the facts.

1.The short version

  1. Answer the questions in section 2. Four of them decide the whole plan: date of death, will or no will, surviving spouse or other children, and what the deed says.
  2. Order a date-of-death appraisal before anyone cleans, repairs, or removes anything. It sets your tax basis and it cannot be reconstructed after the house changes.21
  3. Lock the clocks down this week (section 3): forward the mail, change the locks, call every mortgage servicer, bind vacant-house insurance, enroll in the free county recording alert.
  4. Record a List of Heirs and a Real Estate Affidavit with the Circuit Court Clerk in Abingdon by mail. No probate, no bond, no trip, if you are the sole heir with no will.1
  5. Hire a Virginia estate and real-estate attorney in or near Abingdon (section 9). They prepare the deed and run a mail-away closing.
  6. Pick the selling channel on net proceeds, not headline price: a full listing on the regional MLS, a licensed local auction, or both in sequence. Avoid unsolicited cash offers.
  7. Close by mail-away or Virginia remote online notarization, verify the wire by phone, and file the Virginia nonresident return before the California one.

The one date that matters most

Virginia gives your father's creditors one year from his death to reach the real estate. A sale by an heir inside that year is "not valid against" those creditors, and title insurers treat it as an uninsurable gap.4 If the anniversary is within reach, time the closing past it. If it is not, the realistic options for a sole heir are to qualify as administrator and have the creditor picture adjudicated through a debts-and-demands proceeding, or to negotiate an indemnity or escrow holdback with the title underwriter.5 Find the exact date of death first.

3.This week, in this order

3.1Before anything touches the property

  1. Order a retrospective date-of-death appraisal from a Virginia-licensed residential appraiser, effective date set to the day he died. Keep the report permanently.
  2. Photograph and video everything, inside, outside, every outbuilding. Contents, condition, meters.
  3. Let no one in except the appraiser and a locksmith until those two are done. Do not clean out, repair, list, or sign anything.

3.2The clocks already running

  1. Forward the mail to San Diego. Change the mailing address of record with the Washington County Treasurer and Commissioner of the Revenue. The next tax bill mails to the house on November 20.
  2. Change the locks. Post no-trespassing. Give keys only against a written key receipt.
  3. Call every mortgage servicer. Ask verbatim: "Is this a HECM or reverse mortgage? Has a due-and-payable notice issued, and on what date?" Send a written successor-in-interest notice.
  4. Enroll in the county's free recording alert at virginiapropertynotification.org under your father's name, your name, and the parcel. Vacant, paid-off, inherited houses are the deed-fraud target profile.33
  5. Bind a vacant-dwelling policy in your name or the estate's. The existing policy's named insured is deceased and the house is vacant; assume it will not pay.
  6. Report the death to Social Security if the funeral home did not, flag the three credit bureaus, and pull identity documents out of the house. Fraudulent debt in his name becomes a creditor claim that reaches the land.
  7. Winterize before the first hard freeze. The median first 32°F freeze at the Abingdon weather station is October 20. Drain the system or keep heat on at 55°F or above; Virginia's freeze exclusion applies only when neither was done.

3.3Weeks one to three: put ownership on the record

  1. Get certified death certificates, several.
  2. Pull the recorded deed and read the tenancy words. The deed is behind the county's paid land-records portal, so the attorney or a title company pulls it.
  3. Order a full title examination back at least 60 years: judgments, tax liens, survivorship language, severed mineral rights, prior deeds of gift.
  4. If there is no will, file the List of Heirs (form CC-1611) and the Real Estate Affidavit (form CC-1612) with the Clerk. If there is a will, record the will instead. Either fixes record title, entitles you to foreclosure notice if there is a loan, and puts the true owners in the land records against deed fraud.1
  5. Count from the date of death to the 12-month mark and diary it.

3.4Weeks two to six: the two questions that can eat the equity

  1. Resolve Medicaid. Request a written claim determination from Virginia DMAS before you contract with a buyer.
  2. Decide the selling capacity: you as heir in your own name, or the estate through a qualified personal representative. That choice drives the creditor window, the disclosure exemption, whose taxpayer number goes on the 1099-S, and whether you owe probate tax.

4.3Do you have to open probate at all?

In the common case, no, and you should not want to. An intestate administrator has no power over the real estate anyway, so qualifying buys authority you already have while adding probate tax on the full estate value ($0.10 per $100 over $15,000, § 58.1-1712), a surety bond because you are a nonresident, and sixteen months of inventories and accountings before the Commissioner of Accounts.6 The small-estate affidavit does not reach real estate at all, § 64.2-600.

A California resident can qualify if it comes to that, § 64.2-1426(A), but must file a written consent to service of process on the Clerk, and "bond with surety shall be required in every case" unless a Virginia resident co-fiduciary qualifies at the same time.6 The Washington County Clerk's own probate page says the executor "must personally appear to probate the will and qualify as executor." No statute requires that; it reads as local practice, and probate is absent from the Clerk's by-mail services list. The List of Heirs, the Real Estate Affidavit, and land records are all on the by-mail and e-recording lists, which is a second reason to stay on the no-qualification route.8

4.4What a title company will want before insuring a sale by an heir

  • More than one year since the date of death, § 64.2-534(B), or an adjudicated creditor picture, or an indemnity the underwriter accepts.
  • No administration commenced and no debts-and-demands report filed.
  • Recorded List of Heirs and Real Estate Affidavit; every heir signing; death certificate.
  • Judgment and lien search on your father and on each heir.
  • Payoff of any mortgage or deed of trust. The lien follows the land and comes out of your proceeds.
  • Seller affidavit on debts, possession, and mechanics' liens.

Separately from the land, § 64.2-534(A) makes an heir who sells personally liable, up to the value of the real estate with interest, to people entitled to be paid from it. Ask the attorney how long that exposure lasts.

4.6Disclosure: the exemption you probably do not get

Virginia is a buyer-beware state. The seller hands over a Real Estate Board statement listing matters the buyer is told to investigate for himself: condition, lot lines, adjacent parcels, septic, flood, easements, radon and the rest of a 19-item list. The owner represents nothing on it; there is no California-style condition questionnaire. The version taking effect 1 January 2027 adds land-use and roll-back tax.9 Deliver it before the contract is ratified; delivered late, it gives the buyer a free termination right, § 55.1-709. The exemption for estates covers "transfers by a fiduciary in the course of the administration of a decedent's estate," § 55.1-702(A)(3). An heir selling in his own name is not a fiduciary, so plan on delivering the statement and do not let anyone tell you an inherited house is exempt. A 1920 house also carries the federal lead-paint disclosure and pamphlet, which no Virginia exemption excuses.10 The one disclosure that survives every exemption is prior methamphetamine manufacture, § 55.1-708.

5.Signing from San Diego

Nothing in the Code requires the seller to attend settlement. The normal Virginia pattern for an out-of-state seller is a mail-away closing: the settlement agent overnights the package, you sign, it goes back overnight, they record and wire.

  • A California notary works. Virginia gives an out-of-state notarial act "the same effect as if performed by a notary public of the Commonwealth," § 55.1-616(B)(1), accepts a certificate in the form of the place where it was taken, § 55.1-619, and puts any statement of representative capacity on the signer, not the notary, § 55.1-620.7
  • The catch is the form. California bars its notaries from completing any certificate that asks the notary to determine or certify the signer's capacity, Cal. Civ. Code § 1189(c). If the settlement agent's certificate is worded that way, the fix is the California all-purpose acknowledgment or Virginia remote online notarization, which is deemed performed in Virginia, § 47.1-13(D).
  • Settle the notarization question in writing before the closing date, with the settlement agent and the buyer's lender, not on signing day.
  • A power of attorney is rarely worth it here. If used, it must be recorded, so it must be notarized (§ 64.2-1603, § 55.1-600), and title underwriters typically want it property-specific, original, recent, and pre-approved before you sign it. Signing your own documents avoids the rejection risk.
  • The Clerk e-records. Washington County accepts Simplifile and CSC, so the deed can record the day it is signed. A paper fallback is mandatory regardless, § 17.1-223(C).
  • Who prepares the deed: a Virginia attorney. Virginia deeds carry a preparer statement on the first page, § 17.1-223(B), and the recitals about how you took title have to be right. Ask for a special warranty deed, not a general warranty.
  • The buyer picks the settlement agent, § 55.1-1006. Your right is to have your own attorney, who then has to consent in writing before the buyer's agent charges you fees.

7.Taxes

7.1Bottom line

Your basis resets to the fair market value on the date of death, 26 U.S.C. § 1014(a)(1), and the holding period is long-term automatically, § 1223(9).20 A house sold within months of death, net of a commission and closing costs, usually shows a small long-term capital loss. That loss is deductible, up to $3,000 a year against other income with the rest carried forward, because IRS Publication 559 treats an inherited residence held for sale as investment property "even though it was the decedent's personal residence and even if you didn't rent it out."22 The county's assessed value does not count: Treasury Regulation § 20.2031-1(b) says property "shall not be returned at the value at which it is assessed for local tax purposes unless that value represents the fair market value."21

No federal estate tax: the 2026 exclusion is $15,000,000.23 No Virginia estate or inheritance tax.24 No California estate tax. Virginia does not withhold anything from a nonresident seller at closing.25

7.4Tax traps

  • No date-of-death appraisal. The basis becomes a guess, and the guess gets harder and more expensive to defend the longer you wait.
  • 1099-S taxpayer number not matching the deed. If the deed says "Estate of" but the 1099-S carries your SSN, or the reverse, the IRS matches proceeds against a return that never reported them. Fix it with the settlement agent before closing.
  • Letting anyone live there rent-free. Converts the house to personal-use property; the loss deduction disappears.
  • Filing California's Schedule S and skipping Virginia. Wrong on both sides. The credit lives on Virginia Form 763.
  • Selling through the estate and leaving it open. A loss stays inside the estate until its final year.
  • Renting it out first. Starts depreciation, adds Virginia-source rental income and a Virginia return, and turns part of any later gain into 25% recapture.

Keep: the appraisal, death certificates, the settlement statement, the 1099-S, every receipt for money spent on the house after death, and your father's last returns.

8.2Timing

Nationally and on the East Coast the strongest listing window is April through June; late-April listings have sold for about 4% over the yearly median and were the most likely to go over asking.54 A fall listing lands outside that window. Waiting for spring 2027 buys the better window at the cost of six or seven more months of vacancy, insurance, heat, and risk, and the one-year creditor rule may make the wait unavoidable anyway. Decide with the date of death in hand.

8.3Preparation that pays

  • Pre-listing inspections on the systems that kill rural deals: septic (pump and inspect, $300 to $650 for transaction grade), the water supply (public here, per the county, but confirm), roof, heat, termite and moisture. Fixing or disclosing first removes the buyer's retrade lever.
  • Financed buyers set the bar. FHA, VA and USDA loans all require sound well and septic where present, no peeling paint on a pre-1978 house, and a serviceable roof. Meadowview is almost certainly USDA-eligible; check the exact address on the USDA map.
  • Clean out, paint, mow, photograph professionally. Professionally photographed listings have sold faster and for more in every large study; whole-house paint and curb-appeal work are the highest-return prep on a modest house.
  • Price to the market from day one. Zillow's research on overpricing found that the longer a listing sits, the further below list it finally sells, up to about 12% for the slowest. Rural comps are thin, so the agent's comparative analysis should show its adjustments for age, layout, and condition, not just square footage.
  • Carrying costs while it sits: a vacant-dwelling policy (10 to 25% over a standard premium where an endorsement is offered), electricity for heat, water off and drained, lawn, and someone local to walk through every two weeks.

9.2Questions that separate the honest from the smooth

  1. How many estate or absentee-owner sales have you closed in Washington County in the last two years? Give me two of those sellers as references.
  2. Walk me through a mail-away closing and a remote-online-notarization closing you have done. Who prepared the deed?
  3. What is your fee, in writing, and what is negotiable? What is the listing term, the cancellation terms, and is there an automatic renewal or a protection period?
  4. Will you decline dual agency for this listing, in writing? Will you disclose any interest you, your brokerage, or a relative has in any buyer?
  5. Full MLS entry and syndication on day one, and a written showing report every week?
  6. How will you price it, and can I see the comparative analysis with the adjustments spelled out?
  7. How do you handle the lockbox, showings, and the utilities on a vacant house?
  8. Who is your errors-and-omissions carrier?
  9. For the attorney: will you serve as Virginia co-fiduciary if I have to qualify, and at what fee? Will you get the underwriter to pre-approve any power of attorney before I sign it?

9.3Walk away if

  • They push a quick cash offer, especially one from an affiliate.
  • They ask you to sign a quitclaim deed "to clear up title."
  • They want money by wire or gift card before any service, or send wire instructions by email.
  • They will not put the fee in writing, or propose a net listing.
  • They cannot be found in the DPOR, State Bar, or SCC lookup, or the license is inactive or in someone else's name.
  • They offer a "consultant" or "title expert" who is not licensed as anything.

12.Questions for the pros

12.1For the Virginia attorney

  1. From the recorded deed, was this in my father's sole name, or joint, life estate, transfer-on-death, or trust? Does the estate own it at all?
  2. Given the date of death, has the § 64.2-534 one-year period run? If not: wait, qualify and adjudicate the debts, or an underwriter indemnity, and what does each cost?
  3. Who are the heirs under § 64.2-200 and what fraction does each own? Any risk of an unknown heir?
  4. Do I need to qualify at all, or do I sell as heir with a recorded List of Heirs and Real Estate Affidavit?
  5. If I must qualify, will you serve as resident co-fiduciary so I avoid the surety bond, and at what fee?
  6. What is my personal exposure under § 64.2-534(A) and how long does it last?
  7. Was my father on Medicaid, and how is estate recovery cleared at closing?
  8. Am I exempt from the Residential Property Disclosure Act, or not? I believe not, selling in my own name.
  9. Will you prepare the deed with a special warranty, and can everything be done by mail-away or RON?
  10. Will you represent me under § 55.1-1006 so the buyer's settlement agent cannot charge me without your consent?
  11. Your fee, flat or hourly, and an estimate.

12.2For the Clerk's office

  1. Can an out-of-state administrator or executor qualify by mail or video, or is personal appearance required?
  2. Has anything been filed on my father's estate? Is a will lodged for safekeeping? Can I get the whole file?
  3. Which form do you use for the § 64.2-510 Real Estate Affidavit?
  4. Has the county adopted the local probate tax add-on?
  5. Do you accept e-recording through Simplifile or CSC, and deeds notarized by remote online notarization?
  6. Current fees to record a deed, a List of Heirs, an affidavit; payment from out of state; where the recorded original is mailed.

12.3For the settlement agent, once there is a buyer

  1. Which underwriter, and exactly what do you require to insure a sale by an heir in this posture, inside or outside the one-year window?
  2. California-notarized deed, or RON? Whose platform?
  3. A written estimated seller net sheet: grantor's tax, recording, commission, prorations.
  4. Your wire-fraud verification procedure, and the phone number I should call back, taken from your license record.
  5. Confirm in writing the deed is held until cleared funds are received.
