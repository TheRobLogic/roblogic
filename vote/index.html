The field's been narrowed. Now: the race for governor.

Gavin Newsom is termed out. On June 2, Californians began narrowing a 14-name field to two for the November 3 ballot. Xavier Becerra (D) has effectively secured a spot. The other one is still being counted — Steve Hilton (R) leads the raw tally, Becerra is projected to pass him, and Tom Steyer (D) is close enough that an all-Democrat November remains possible. Below: who's in the running, what they've done, and what the news says — sourced, no spin.

151 days until the November 3, 2026 general election.
Result not yet called

As of June 5, 2026, the June 2 top-two primary is still being counted and the Associated Press has not projected the two finalists. Xavier Becerra (D) has effectively secured a place in November — Decision Desk HQ projects him advancing and expects him to overtake Hilton for first as Democratic-leaning mail ballots are tallied. The second spot is a genuine contest between Steve Hilton (R) and Tom Steyer (D); if the late ballots break far enough toward Steyer, the November ballot could feature two Democrats and no Republican.

Who's in the running

Two living dossiers — who they are, what they've done, what the news says, and what they're doing now. Becerra is in; Hilton currently holds the second slot but is contested by Steyer. Updated as the race develops.

Xavier Becerra Dem
Advancing to November (projected). Former U.S. Secretary of Health & Human Services; former California Attorney General; 12-term U.S. Representative.

Who he is

Xavier Becerra, 68 (born Jan. 26, 1958, in Sacramento), is a Democrat and the son of working-class Mexican-American parents — the first in his family to graduate college (Stanford B.A. 1980, J.D. 1984). He was the first Latino to serve as California Attorney General and the first Latino to lead the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. He has been out of government since leaving the Biden cabinet on Jan. 20, 2025. Wikipedia · House History

What he's done

  • U.S. House of Representatives, Los Angeles (1993–2017). Twelve terms; chaired the House Democratic Caucus (2013–2017), the No. 4 House Democratic leadership post. House History
  • California Attorney General (2017–2021). Appointed by Gov. Jerry Brown to the seat Kamala Harris vacated, then elected in 2018. His office filed more than 120 lawsuits against the first Trump administration — concentrated on the environment, immigration, and health care — and led a 21-state coalition defending DACA, which the Supreme Court ruled had been unlawfully rescinded. CalMatters
  • U.S. HHS Secretary (2021–2025). Confirmed March 18, 2021 by 50–49 (Sen. Susan Collins the only Republican voting yes). Oversaw the later COVID-19 response, implementation of the Affordable Care Act, and federal abortion-access policy after the 2022 Dobbs decision. NPR
  • Scrutiny (attributed). Per New York Times reporting (2023–24), HHS failed to re-contact roughly 85,000 unaccompanied migrant children placed with sponsors during his tenure; House Homeland Security subpoenaed him in September 2024. Becerra disputes the "lost 85,000 kids" framing, saying HHS policy at the time limited post-placement contact authority. background

What the news says

Campaign-finance matter (he is the victim, not charged). Per the U.S. Department of Justice (Eastern District of California) and CalMatters, about $225,000 was diverted between 2022 and 2024 from Becerra's dormant state campaign account to his longtime chief of staff, Sean McCluskie. Dana Williamson — Gov. Newsom's former chief of staff — pleaded guilty to conspiracy, filing a false tax return, and making false statements; McCluskie and lobbyist Greg Campbell also pleaded guilty. Prosecutors treat Becerra as the victim; he is not charged. Becerra: "As I said from Day One, I was not involved, I did nothing wrong. And now the record confirms it." Sentencing was postponed from June 4 to Aug. 6, 2026. CalMatters · SLO Tribune

Endorsements & framing. The California Medical Association endorsed him (April 28, 2026), while the nurses' union and other single-payer advocates backed Steyer. TIME called Becerra an "unexpected frontrunner"; CalMatters called the contest the "most uncertain California governor's race in years." KQED · TIME · CalMatters

What he's doing now

  • Message. Running on "Build, Protect, and Lead California," he leans on his record over promises: "I am not running on inflated promises… I am running on my record." His central contrast is anti-Trump competence, pointing to the more than 120 lawsuits he filed as AG. campaign
  • Stated priorities. Health care and Medi-Cal; housing as "essential infrastructure"; affordability (anti-price-gouging, child care, prescription drugs); a pledge to freeze utility and home-insurance rates while pursuing longer-term fixes; first-time-homebuyer assistance; and defense of abortion, voting, and LGBTQ+ rights. priorities
  • Single-payer shift (chronology). He testified for single-payer in 1994 and posted on March 23, 2026 that he was "ready to go further as Governor and deliver single-payer." Before the CMA's April 28 endorsement, the CMA's president said Becerra stated he "wasn't supportive of single payer" "at this point." His campaign (spokesperson Jonathan Underland): "A single payer system is the right goal — but it's not possible under this administration," citing reliance on federal waivers. KQED
Steve Hilton Rep
Currently 2nd in the count — the second slot is contested by Tom Steyer (D). Former Fox News host; former senior adviser to UK Prime Minister David Cameron.

Who he is

Steve Hilton, 56 (born Aug. 25, 1969, in London), is a Republican and a British-American — the son of Hungarian immigrants who fled the 1956 revolution (the family anglicized its surname from Hircsák). He became a U.S. citizen in May 2021 and renounced his British citizenship in 2025. He holds a B.A. from New College, Oxford, and moved to California in 2012. Wikipedia

What he's done

  • Director of Strategy to UK PM David Cameron (2010–2012). A senior No. 10 adviser credited with shaping Cameron's "Big Society" agenda. Wikipedia
  • Fox News host (2017–2023). Hosted the weekly program "The Next Revolution," then remained a Fox News contributor. Wikipedia
  • Tech & policy. Co-founded the political crowdfunding platform Crowdpac (resigned as CEO in May 2018); founded the California policy group Golden Together in 2023 with Lanhee Chen and Gloria Romero. Semafor
  • Author. Books include More Human (2015) and Positive Populism (2018). He casts himself as a populist conservative running against one-party rule, under the banner "Make California Golden Again." campaign

What the news says

How he led the primary. Hilton led early returns at about 27% and consolidated the Republican vote (PPIC found roughly 53% of Republicans backed him, vs. ~33% for Chad Bianco). Decision Desk HQ nonetheless projects Becerra advancing and expects him to pass Hilton as mail ballots are counted; the second slot, between Hilton and Steyer, was uncalled as of June 4. PPIC · The Hill

Endorsements & the deep-blue math (attributed). Trump endorsed Hilton on April 6, 2026 ("COMPLETE & TOTAL ENDORSEMENT"), credited with sidelining rival Republican Chad Bianco; VP JD Vance endorsed on primary day. Democrats make up about 45% of registered California voters to Republicans' ~25%, and no Republican has won the governorship since Schwarzenegger's 2006 re-election; analysts quoted by ABC7 called the Trump tie an "albatross" statewide, and prediction markets around the endorsement put Hilton's odds of winning the governorship near 6%. CNN · SF Standard · Newsweek

Post-primary friction. On June 4 Hilton criticized California's slow count, calling the election system "another monument to their uselessness," and cited a whistleblower claim about ballot handling (an unverified allegation). Townhall

What he's doing now

  • Message. Post-primary: "Nothing is final yet, but it does look as if change is coming to California." His theory of the race is affordability and competence versus one-party rule — winning over disaffected Democrats and independents on cost of living. ABC7
  • Taxes. No state income tax on earnings under $100,000 and a 7.5% flat tax above that (his campaign says this removes roughly 7.1 million households from the income-tax rolls); protect Prop 13. campaign
  • Cost of living & housing. Lower gas and electricity prices ("Cali-ffordability"); cut housing impact fees and regulations, defend single-family zoning. KEYT
  • Homelessness, crime, energy. Move people off the streets (state intervention where localities fail) paired with treatment, citing roughly $24B spent "without any idea where it went"; expand jail capacity and back law enforcement; "abundant, affordable energy," including more drilling. Times of San Diego · CalMatters
