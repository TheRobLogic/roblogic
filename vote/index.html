Gavin Newsom can't run again. That should be the simple headline. California Democrats have a 23-point registration advantage, haven't lost a statewide race since 2006, and have roughly a dozen plausible succession candidates on their bench. In any normal cycle, this is a coronation with a primary speed bump.

Instead, on April 18, 2026 — 45 days out from the June 2 primary — the Democratic field is splintered across eight candidates who can't break 20% each, the party's frontrunner just resigned from Congress over sexual assault allegations, Newsom and Pelosi have publicly announced they won't endorse anyone, and the two leading Republicans are quietly the best-polling candidates in the race.

Because California uses a top-two primary — all candidates on one ballot regardless of party; the top two advance to November — there is a real, mathematical scenario where the November 2026 ballot for governor has no Democrat on it. Democratic strategists put that scenario at 15–27% probability depending on the day.

The Democratic Party had fifteen years to build a bench and produced this. Gavin Newsom's successor may well be wearing red.

§1The Swalwell Implosion

TL;DR: In under 96 hours between Friday April 10 and Monday April 13, Rep. Eric Swalwell went from leading Democratic contender for California governor to a resigned ex-congressman facing a Manhattan DA investigation, a House Ethics probe, and a growing roster of accusers. By April 15, a fifth woman had come forward on the record.

Timeline

  • Friday April 10 — San Francisco Chronicle and CNN publish near-simultaneous investigations. The Chronicle details allegations from a former Castro Valley district staffer (hired at 21 in 2019); CNN surfaces three additional women alleging unsolicited explicit messages, nude photos, and unwanted advances. Swalwell calls the reports "flat false."
  • Saturday April 11 — Manhattan DA confirms it is investigating the staffer's 2024 New York allegation.
  • Sunday April 12 — Swalwell suspends his campaign: "I am deeply sorry for mistakes in judgment I've made in my past. I will fight the serious, false allegations that have been made — but that's my fight, not a campaign's." 55 former staffers sign an open letter calling the reporting "serious, credible" and demanding he resign from Congress.
  • Monday April 13 — Swalwell resigns from Congress, effective Tuesday. House Ethics opens an investigation the same morning.
  • Tuesday April 15 — Lonna Drewes holds an LA press conference alleging Swalwell drugged and raped her in a West Hollywood hotel in 2018 under the pretense of retrieving "papers." She is the fifth accuser on the record. Her attorney says more have made contact.

The Collapse

Within hours of the Friday reports, the support floor dropped out. Campaign chair Rep. Jimmy Gomez resigned his role and publicly called for Swalwell to exit. Sens. Gallego, Schiff, Padilla all pulled support Friday afternoon. Reps. Ted Lieu, Doris Matsui, Mike Thompson rescinded endorsements. The California Teachers Association and SEIU suspended their backing. Pelosi reportedly called Swalwell Sunday urging resignation.

Ballot Reality Swalwell suspended after the statutory withdrawal deadline, so his name will still appear on the June 2 ballot. Any votes he receives are effectively wasted — a real risk given the compressed timeline and vote-by-mail ballots already en route May 4.

Prior Baggage, for Context

Fang Fang (2020): Swalwell had a long-running association with Christine Fang, a suspected Chinese intelligence operative who cultivated Bay Area politicians from his Dublin city council days and bundled for his early House campaigns. FBI gave him a defensive briefing in 2015; House Ethics closed a probe with no action. In March 2026, FBI Director Kash Patel pushed to release the old investigative file; Swalwell sent a cease-and-desist.

Trump impeachment manager (2020): Swalwell served as one of the House managers in the second Trump impeachment trial — which raised his national profile, made him a persistent Trump-administration target, and is the context for Patel's March 2026 push to release the Fang files.

Rep. Sam Liccardo (D-CA) told ABC7 on April 14 that rumors of Swalwell's misconduct had "swirled for years" in California political circles — a claim now being cited by House Ethics and the Manhattan DA as a starting point.

§6How It Shakes Out

Three scenarios for the June 2 top-two primary. Probabilities as of April 18, 45 days out.

Scenario 1 · R-vs-R November ballot

~15–20% probability

Both Hilton and Bianco finish top-2. The Dem field stays fractured. Combined GOP share ticks from 31% to 34–36% via late undecided consolidation in Central Valley / Inland Empire.

What pushes toward this: Scandal/collapse for Steyer or Becerra before May 4 VBM drop. Low-polling Dems refuse to quit. Opposition research drop on the Dem frontrunner.

November dynamics: A guaranteed Democratic governor — California hasn't elected a statewide Republican since 2006. Race becomes a GOP ideology referendum: Hilton (Trump-aligned) vs. Bianco (Oath Keeper). Dem turnout collapses, hurting down-ballot House districts (CA-22, CA-27, CA-41, CA-45). Hilton likely beats Bianco ~58–42 in general.

Scenario 2 · R-vs-D November ballot (most likely — Hilton + Steyer or Hilton + Becerra)

~70–75% probability

Hilton consolidates the GOP to 20–23% and finishes first. One Democrat breaks from the pack to 16–19% and takes second. Bianco lands third at 12–14%.

Who's the Dem? Steyer is likeliest — self-funds at unlimited scale, CTA endorsement, leads voters under 50. Becerra is the live alternative — gained most from Swalwell exit, statewide name ID, can consolidate Latinos. Porter is fading — "disastrous week" coverage, slipping polls.

Catalysts: April 22 debate (all 9 remaining on stage — breakout moment decides second place). Low-polling Dems quitting and endorsing. Steyer dumping $20–30M on broadcast in the final two weeks.

November dynamics: Democrat wins 15–20 points. Steyer wins 58–42 (climate + affordability). Becerra wins 60–40 (healthcare + experience). Porter wins narrower 55–45 (weaker general-election debater than her CFPB rep suggests).

Scenario 3 · D-vs-D November ballot

~5–10% probability

Simultaneous collapse of both Republicans. Combined GOP share falls from 31% to 22–24%. Two Democrats consolidate into high teens — likeliest pairing Steyer (19%) + Becerra (17%).

What pushes toward this: Federal indictment/civil finding against Bianco; damaging Trump-Hilton joint appearance; CAGOP split where Bianco's 49% convention supporters openly sabotage Hilton; a late unifying Newsom/Obama endorsement (unlikely but decisive).

November dynamics: Policy race between two Democrats — Steyer's wealth-tax populism vs. Becerra's institutional experience. Republican turnout craters. Slight edge to Becerra head-to-head (Steyer's billionaire profile cuts against his populism one-on-one).

The cross-cutting factors

Trump's Hilton endorsement splits, doesn't consolidate. The convention vote (Bianco 49, Hilton 44) is the tell: Bianco's base sees Hilton as a carpetbagging Fox personality, and Trump's thumb on the scale activated anti-establishment GOP grievance. Net effect: +3–5 pts for Hilton with MAGA-aligned indies, −2–3 pts with Bianco-loyal grassroots. Helps Hilton finish first, hurts combined GOP total.

Unifying Dem endorsement is not coming. Newsom's April 15 statement ("I have full confidence voters will choose…") explicitly declined. Historical precedent: Brown stayed out of 2018 Newsom-Villaraigosa. Absence is priced in. A late Obama nod would lock Scenario 2 and decide which Democrat advances. Don't bet on it.

Turnout. Non-presidential June primaries run 35–40%, skewing older, whiter, higher-propensity — which helps Republicans (Hilton leads over-50 at 24%) and hurts fragmented Democrats. Undecideds (23%) break late and toward name-ID + TV saturation. Steyer's self-funding is the clearest late-break advantage; Hilton's Fox tenure is second.

§7The Bottom Line

The most likely outcome, consensus view as of April 18:

  1. June 2 primary: Hilton finishes first (~19%). Steyer or Becerra finishes second (~16–18%). Bianco third (~13%).
  2. November general: A Democrat wins by 12–20 points. California doesn't elect Republicans statewide.
  3. The 20% risk: Dem field stays fractured through May 4 VBM drop and both Republicans advance. In that world, Hilton becomes governor. That's the real stakes of this primary.

The Democratic Party has 45 days to consolidate. The tools — one or two low-polling Dems dropping out and endorsing, a Newsom statement (won't happen), a Steyer blitz on broadcast — are sitting on the table. The question is whether the party can pick them up in time.

If California ends up with a Republican governor in January 2027, it won't be because voters wanted one. It'll be because the Democratic field refused to do subtraction.

CEO Briefing.

Two-minute read. What matters, what to watch, where the risk is.

Bottom line up front Democrats will likely hold the CA governorship in November 2026, but with a meaningful (15–20%) tail risk of losing it to a Republican because the D field is too fractured to clear top-two primary math. Election June 2; 45 days remaining.

The asset at stake

California governor controls the fifth-largest economy in the world, $320B state budget, judicial appointments, and a bully pulpit on federal immigration / environmental / labor policy. Losing this seat would be a 20-year outlier.

The structural issue

California uses a top-two, non-partisan primary. Eight Democrats are splitting a 40–45% Democratic-leaning electorate. Two Republicans are consolidating a 25% GOP electorate. On current polling, the math works: Hilton (R) leads at 17%, and if no Democrat breaks 15%, two Republicans advance.

Three base-case watches

  • April 22 debate: All 9 remaining on stage. Breakout moment likely decides second place. Watch Becerra.
  • April 23 fundraising deadline: Q1 2026 filings. Low-polling candidates with no cash face pressure to quit and endorse.

  • Late Obama/Pelosi endorsement. Unlikely but decisive. Would lock a Dem into the runoff.
  • Second scandal. Another Swalwell-style collapse — most exposed: Porter (staffer complaints, ex-husband filings), Villaraigosa (Herbalife, old affair), Becerra (HHS migrant-children record).
  • Bianco legal event. He's asking donors to pay legal bills. A federal indictment or DOJ finding on the Riverside jail investigation would tank the GOP share and push toward Scenario 3 (D-vs-D).

The two-line takeaway

Democrats should win but aren't acting like it. The Republican field is a joke everywhere except the only math that matters — which is how many Democrats there are.

ELI13.

You're in 8th grade. You know how elections work. Here's what's weird about this one.

California picks a new governor every four years. Gavin Newsom has been governor for 8 years, which is the maximum — he has to leave in January 2027. So this June, Californians vote in a primary to start picking his replacement.

Here's the twist that makes California different: everyone runs on the same ballot. In most states, Democrats have their own primary and Republicans have their own primary. In California, all 61 candidates — Democrats, Republicans, Greens, everyone — are on one ballot, and whichever two get the most votes advance to the November election. Doesn't matter what party they're from.

Usually this doesn't cause problems because usually one Democrat and one Republican finish top-two, and then in November the Democrat wins because California has way more Democrats than Republicans (about twice as many).

But this year is weird.

There are eight major Democrats running — billionaires, ex-Congress people, mayors, a schools superintendent — and they're all splitting the Democratic vote. Nobody is winning. The highest-polling Democrat is only at 14%.

Meanwhile there are only two Republicans running, and they're at 17% and 14%. Higher than every Democrat.

So mathematically, it's possible that in June, both Republicans will get more votes than any single Democrat, and they'll both advance to November. That would mean the November ballot has no Democrat on it at all. And then no matter who Californians pick, they get a Republican governor. In a state that's voted Democratic for 20 years.

This is called "spoiling your own primary," and it's happening because the Democrats can't agree on one person to back.

It got worse last week. The Democrat who was leading — Eric Swalwell, a congressman from the Bay Area — was accused by five women of sexual assault. He dropped out of the race on Sunday and resigned from Congress on Monday. His supporters are now spread across the other seven Democrats, which makes the splitting problem even worse.

The two Republicans are not great either.

Steve Hilton is a former Fox News host who used to work for the British Prime Minister. He became an American citizen in 2021. Donald Trump endorsed him two weeks ago.

Chad Bianco is a sheriff from Riverside County. He used to belong to a militia group called the Oath Keepers (some members were involved in the January 6 Capitol riot). His jails have had more deaths than any other county. Last week he seized 650,000 ballots from a different election because he claimed there was fraud.

These are the two candidates who might win the governorship of California.

What will probably happen? Most likely — maybe 70% chance — one Democrat will break out of the pack and make it to November, and then beat a Republican in the general election. Democrats always beat Republicans in California because there are so many more of them.

But there's a 15–20% chance the Democrats stay splintered, both Republicans advance, and the next governor of California is a Republican for the first time in 25 years.

That's the whole story. 45 days until the primary.

ELI5.

You're five. Here's the story with no big words.

California is a big place with a boss called the governor. The boss has to leave soon because he's been boss a long time.

Now people want to be the new boss. One team — the Blue team — has EIGHT people who want to be boss. The other team — the Red team — has only two.

The rule is: everyone counts up their votes, and the top two get to be in the big final. It doesn't matter what team they're on.

California is mostly a Blue state. Usually Blue wins. But this time Blue has too many people wanting the same job, and they're all sharing their votes with each other. Nobody has very many.

The two Red team people don't have to share their votes with as many people, so they have more.

Most grown-ups think Blue will still win. But nobody is sure. The vote is in 45 days.
