Jump to The Swalwell Collapse The Democratic Field The Republican Field Where They Stand Follow the Money How It Shakes Out The Bottom Line

Gavin Newsom can't run again. That should be the simple headline. California Democrats have a roughly 20-point registration advantage (Dec 2025 SoS 154-day report: 45.0% D / 25.1% R / 22.7% NPP / 7.3% other), haven't seen a Republican win a statewide race since 2006, and have multiple plausible succession candidates. In any normal cycle, this is a coronation with a primary speed bump.

But Kamala Harris announced in July 2025 that she would not run, and Sen. Alex Padilla has also declined — removing the two candidates most capable of clearing the Democratic field. As of April 29, 2026 — 34 days from the June 2 primary — the Democratic field is splintered across seven active candidates (none polling above the mid-teens), the candidate who had been leading in March (Swalwell) resigned from Congress on April 14 after sexual-misconduct allegations, and the two leading Republicans are currently the best-polling candidates in the race.

Because California uses a top-two primary — all candidates on one ballot regardless of party; the top two advance to November — there is a real, mathematical scenario in which the November 2026 ballot has no Democrat on it. Paul Mitchell's model put that probability at 27% before Swalwell exited; post-Swalwell estimates from Mitchell and other California strategists now run closer to 15–20%.

With Harris and Padilla on the sidelines and Swalwell out, the Democratic field is a fractured second tier. Gavin Newsom's successor could plausibly be wearing red.

§1The Swalwell Collapse

TL;DR: Between Friday April 10 and Tuesday April 14, Rep. Eric Swalwell went from leading Democratic contender for California governor to a resigned ex-congressman facing a Manhattan DA investigation and a growing roster of accusers. Swalwell has denied the allegations. A fifth accuser came forward publicly on April 15.

Timeline

  • Friday April 10 — San Francisco Chronicle and CNN publish near-simultaneous reports. The Chronicle reports allegations from a former Castro Valley district-office staffer (hired in 2019 at age 21) alleging two non-consensual encounters beginning that year. CNN separately reports allegations from four women, including a 2024 New York hotel rape allegation from a different former staffer, plus three women alleging unsolicited explicit messages and advances. Swalwell calls the reports "flat false."
  • Saturday April 11 — Manhattan DA confirms an investigation into the 2024 New York hotel allegation.
  • Sunday April 12 — Swalwell suspends his campaign: "I am deeply sorry for mistakes in judgment I've made in my past. I will fight the serious, false allegations that have been made — but that's my fight, not a campaign's." An open letter signed by 50+ former staffers calls the reporting "serious, credible" and demands he resign from Congress.
  • Monday April 13 — Swalwell announces he will resign from Congress.
  • Tuesday April 14 — Resignation effective. The House Ethics Committee had opened an inquiry that same week but closed it upon his resignation (the committee has no jurisdiction over former members).
  • Tuesday April 15 — At a Los Angeles press conference (covered by AP, the LA Times, and CNN), Lonna Drewes alleges Swalwell drugged and raped her at a West Hollywood hotel in 2018 under the pretense of retrieving "papers." Her attorney says more women have made contact. Swalwell's legal team denies the allegation. The LA County Sheriff's Department opened a rape investigation tied to the Drewes allegation on April 14 (CNBC).
  • Monday April 20 — The House Ethics Committee issued a rare public statement defending its record on sexual-misconduct probes and inviting additional alleged victims to come forward — unusual given the committee has no further jurisdiction over Swalwell.

The Collapse

Within hours of the Friday reports, the support floor dropped out. Campaign chair Rep. Jimmy Gomez resigned his role and publicly called for Swalwell to exit. Sens. Gallego, Schiff, Padilla pulled support Friday afternoon. Reps. Ted Lieu, Doris Matsui, Mike Thompson rescinded endorsements. The California Teachers Association and SEIU California suspended their backing. Pelosi reportedly called Swalwell on Sunday urging resignation.

Ballot Reality Swalwell suspended after the statutory withdrawal deadline, so his name will still appear on the June 2 ballot. Any votes he receives are effectively wasted — a real risk given the compressed timeline and vote-by-mail ballots beginning to mail May 4.

Prior Baggage, for Context

Fang Fang (2020): Swalwell had a prolonged association with Christine Fang, a suspected Chinese intelligence operative who cultivated Bay Area politicians from his Dublin city council days and helped bundle for his early House campaigns. The FBI gave him a defensive briefing in 2015; a House Ethics probe closed in May 2023 with no action. In March 2026, FBI Director Kash Patel pushed to release the old investigative file; Swalwell's legal team responded publicly opposing release.

Trump impeachment manager (2020): Swalwell served as one of the House managers in the second Trump impeachment trial, which raised his national profile and made him a persistent Trump-administration target — context for Patel's March 2026 push on the Fang files.

Rep. Sam Liccardo (D-CA) told ABC7 on April 14 that rumors of Swalwell's misconduct had "swirled for years" in California political circles.

Sources: CNN · NPR · WaPo · CalMatters · SF Standard

§2The Democratic Field

California's bench was supposed to be the deepest in the country. Harris and Padilla opted out; Swalwell collapsed; Kounalakis, Atkins, and Yee have since withdrawn. What remains is a field of seven active candidates, none above the mid-teens.

Hedge-fund founder, climate-first populist. Third statewide campaign; his 2020 presidential run spent ~$253M (mostly self-funded) for no delegates.

Record: ~$132M self-funded as of Apr 27 (CalMatters) — up from ~$122M at the Feb filing. Per published reporting and SEC filings, Farallon Capital — which he founded and left in 2012 — held positions valued at roughly $90M in CoreCivic (private ICE detention) during his tenure; he has called that "a mistake." Farallon also financed $2B+ in Indonesian and Australian coal acquisitions through ~2014. Steyer's public climate advocacy began ~2012 after he left Farallon. Apr 27–28: Steyer launched a pair of attack ads on Becerra over the HHS unaccompanied-minor record; CHIRLA Action Fund held a counter-presser.

Attack-ad raw material: coal and CoreCivic footage; Atherton hedge-fund biography cutting against populist framing.

With Harris and Padilla opting out of the race and Kounalakis, Atkins, Yee, and Swalwell since withdrawing or suspending, California Democrats face a second-tier primary — seven active candidates, none above the mid-teens.

§3The Republican Field

California has not elected a Republican to a statewide office since 2006, but the top-two primary makes intra-GOP math the story of the moment. The two GOP candidates are former Fox News host Steve Hilton and Riverside Sheriff Chad Bianco. Neither cleared the 60% threshold at the April 12 CAGOP convention (Bianco 49, Hilton 44). At current polling, they are the best-polling candidates in the field.

Steve HiltonR · 17% (lead)

Former Fox News host and British political consultant. Spent 11 years at Fox ("The Next Revolution," 2017–2023) after emigrating from the UK. Lives in Atherton, CA.

Record: David Cameron's Director of Strategy 2010–2012 (the "Big Society" / Brexit-era Downing Street). Became a US citizen in May 2021; held dual UK/US citizenship 2021–2024; renounced UK citizenship in 2024 (£492 fee). Wife Rachel Whetstone is CCO at Netflix (ex-Google, ex-Uber). Book: Positive Populism. UK political memoirs describe him as Cameron's "pint-sized Rasputin."

Fundraising: Top Republican fundraiser at ~$7.1M total raised through ~18,000 individual contributions (per April 2026 CalMatters/KRON4 campaign reports; the frequently-cited "30,000 donors" figure reflects the February snapshot). Per CalMatters, Hilton has also self-loaned approximately $200K to his campaign. Sergey Brin contributed the $39,200 individual maximum.

The Trump wrinkle: Trump endorsed him April 6. Six days later, the CAGOP convention declined to endorse — 44% Hilton, 49% Bianco, neither hitting the 60% threshold. Hilton embraced the endorsement at the April 22 debate ("a deep honor") and pitched a "constructive relationship" with the Trump administration on forestry, energy, and fraud. CBS/YouGov Apr 23–27 had him narrowly first overall at 16%, with the top four (Hilton 16 / Steyer 15 / Becerra 13 / Bianco 10) inside a statistical tie.

General-election headwinds: Republicans haven't won statewide since 2006; a ~20-point Dem registration advantage; national-origin rhetoric used against him at CAGOP convention by Bianco allies.

Chad BiancoR · 14%

Riverside County Sheriff. Campaigning on a "law and order" platform. CJCJ reports his department recorded the lowest Part 1 crime-clearance rate of any California sheriff's department from 2019–2024 (9.2% — less than half the state average).

Record: Riverside County jails recorded 18 inmate deaths in 2022, the highest number in 15 years per LAist reporting. The department is under a California DOJ pattern-and-practice investigation opened in February 2023 over jail deaths and use-of-force. A 2014 Oath Keepers membership ($40, one-year) was confirmed by Bianco after a 2021 data leak; he has said he quickly let the membership lapse. In April 2026, Bianco's department seized approximately 650,000 ballots tied to California's redistricting referendum on election-fraud grounds; the California Supreme Court halted the action and unsealed warrants that did not cite direct evidence of fraud. On April 14, the Riverside County Board of Supervisors voted 4–1 to decline paying the department's legal defense. In late April, the CA Supreme Court granted AG Bonta's request for an expedited briefing schedule, fast-tracking the case before the June 2 primary. Bianco endorsed Trump in 2024 in uniform on Instagram ("It's time we put a felon in the White House") — video still circulating.

Fundraising: ~$4.4M cumulative per CA SoS filings, with donor appeals for legal defense. Cumulative raise now trails Hilton's by roughly 2:1.

General-election headwinds: the 650K-ballot seizure footage; DOJ pattern-and-practice investigation; general-election math against a statewide Democratic registration advantage.

The three money stories that matter

1. Steyer is outspending the field. ~$132M self-funded as of Apr 27, effectively uncapped — his 2020 presidential campaign spent ~$253M (mostly his personal funds). Won the California Teachers Association endorsement on April 10 after CTA dropped Swalwell. If TV saturation drives decision-making, Steyer is positioned to take a top-two slot on money alone.

2. The anti-Steyer ad stack has scaled to ~$14M. PG&E routed ~$10M through a feeder, "Californians for Resilient and Affordable Energy," into the public-facing PAC "California Is Not for Sale," joined by the California Association of Realtors ($5M), the California Building Industry Association ($1M), the California Correctional Peace Officers Association ($25K), and IBEW Local 1245. Largest opposition-spending event of the cycle. Steyer responded with a climate-focused ad pivot.

3. Mahan is the "does tech money move California?" test case. ~$25M raised / ~$19.5M spent across two tech-IE PACs (lead vehicle "California Back to Basics" funded by Moritz, Caruso, Lonsdale, Brin, Tan, Baszucki, Huffman, Oberndorf, Sethi, Seibel, Merrill) — Mahan is still polling in the 5% range. An April 2026 FPPC complaint filed by Raul Claros (founder of California Rising) alleges illegal coordination between the Mahan campaign and California Back to Basics, citing an April 6 Zoom fundraising call where Moritz, Caruso, and Mahan participated and the campaign consultant's strategy slides were screen-shared. Investigation is pending; no finding. So far, the cycle's proof-of-concept is that tech-oligarch IE money has not translated at the voter level.

4. The Newsom ghost. Newsom, Pelosi, Harris, Padilla, Biden, Obama, Hillary Clinton, and SEIU California — all publicly silent on a primary endorsement as of April 29. Newsom told reporters Apr 28 he is "supporting a Democrat in the runoff" but declined to back a candidate before June 2. Harris explicitly declined to run in July 2025; Padilla opted out as well. Analysts have speculated Newsom's silence serves both a possible 2028 positioning and a preference not to be seen opposing any eventual Dem successor, but Newsom has given no on-record explanation.

§6How It Shakes Out

Three scenarios for the June 2 top-two primary. Probabilities as of April 29, 34 days out. Numbers are ranges from public polling (Emerson, CBS/YouGov, IVN, CADEM internal) and Paul Mitchell's model, not a single forecaster.

Scenario 1 · R-vs-R November ballot

~15–20% probability

Both Hilton and Bianco finish top-two. The Dem field stays fractured. Combined GOP share rises from current ~31% to 34–36% via late undecided consolidation in the Central Valley and Inland Empire. The April 22 debate did not reshuffle the GOP vote, so this path still requires a new catalyst on the Dem side.

What pushes toward this: A scandal or collapse for Steyer, Becerra, or Porter before the May 4 VBM mailing; low-polling Dems refusing to drop; additional opposition research on a Dem frontrunner.

November dynamics: California has not elected a Republican to a statewide office since 2006. In this scenario the November race becomes a GOP ideology contest (Hilton-Trump-aligned vs. Bianco-Oath-Keeper). Dem turnout would likely decline, which would pressure down-ballot House districts (CA-22, CA-27, CA-41, CA-45). Head-to-head, public polling averages suggest Hilton would beat Bianco comfortably.

Scenario 2 · R-vs-D November ballot (most likely)

~70–75% probability

Hilton consolidates the GOP to 20–23% and finishes first. One Democrat breaks from the pack to 16–19% and takes second. Bianco lands third at 12–14%. As of April 29, the Dem second-place race is a three-way contest — the Democratic frontrunner is not settled.

Which Democrat? Steyer and Becerra are currently co-likeliest. Steyer retains the money advantage (~$132M self-fund, CTA endorsement, decisive broadcast capacity); Becerra has the institutional momentum (Speaker Rivas + 14 Assembly Dems, IVN poll at 23%, CBS/YouGov Apr 27 third at 13%, Yee consolidation cleared from the lane). Porter has labor support (Teamsters, UAW, CWA, NUHW) but trails in money and in the post-debate polling snapshot.

Catalysts already resolved: April 22 debate (six qualified at 5% threshold; no clear winner). April 23 PG&E-led anti-Steyer ad stack launched, scaled to ~$14M by Apr 27. April 27–28 Steyer ran attack ads on Becerra over the HHS unaccompanied-minor record; CHIRLA Action Fund counter-pressed. April 28 second debate on CBS LA / Pomona College — eight on stage (Thurmond and Villaraigosa qualified under a 1% threshold); Becerra absorbed the heaviest fire; no clear winner. Apr 23–27 CBS/YouGov cross-confirmed Becerra third at 13%, ahead of Bianco.

Catalysts still open: May 5 CNN-hosted two-hour debate. May 4 VBM mailing — earliest ballots in voters' hands. Newspaper editorial endorsements (LA Times, SF Chronicle, Sac Bee, SDUT, Fresno Bee — none issued as of Apr 29). Additional low-polling-Dem consolidation. Steyer's May broadcast capacity vs. the on-air ~$14M anti-Steyer ad stack.

November dynamics: Democrat wins by high-single to low-double digits. Head-to-head: Steyer wins on climate + affordability, Becerra wins on healthcare + institutional experience, Porter wins a narrower race.

Scenario 3 · D-vs-D November ballot

~5–10% probability

Simultaneous decline by both Republicans. Combined GOP share falls from ~31% to 22–24%. Two Democrats consolidate into the high teens — likeliest pairing Steyer (~19%) + Becerra (~17%).

What pushes toward this: A federal indictment or civil finding against Bianco; a damaging Trump-Hilton joint appearance; CAGOP Bianco delegates visibly defecting from Hilton; a late Newsom or Obama endorsement (unlikely but decisive).

November dynamics: A policy contest between Steyer's wealth-tax populism and Becerra's institutional experience. Republican turnout would likely crater. Head-to-head, Becerra probably holds a slight edge (Steyer's self-funder profile cuts against populist framing in a one-on-one).

The cross-cutting factors

Trump's Hilton endorsement splits, doesn't consolidate. The convention vote (Bianco 49, Hilton 44) is the tell: Bianco's base sees Hilton as a carpetbagging Fox personality, and Trump's thumb on the scale activated anti-establishment GOP grievance. Net effect: +3–5 pts for Hilton with MAGA-aligned indies, −2–3 pts with Bianco-loyal grassroots. Helps Hilton finish first, hurts combined GOP total.

Unifying Dem endorsement is not coming. Newsom's April 15 statement ("I have full confidence voters will choose…") and his April 28 WaPo signal ("supporting a Democrat in the runoff") both explicitly declined to endorse in the primary. Historical precedent: Brown stayed out of 2018 Newsom-Villaraigosa. Absence is priced in. A late Obama nod would lock Scenario 2 and decide which Democrat advances. Don't bet on it.

Turnout. Non-presidential June primaries run 35–40%, skewing older, whiter, higher-propensity — which helps Republicans (Hilton leads over-50 at 24%) and hurts fragmented Democrats. Undecideds (23%) break late and toward name-ID + TV saturation. Steyer's self-funding is the clearest late-break advantage; Hilton's Fox tenure is second.

§7The Bottom Line

The most likely outcome, consensus view as of April 29:

  1. June 2 primary: Hilton finishes first (~19%). Steyer or Becerra finishes second (~16–18%). Bianco third (~13%).
  2. November general: A Democrat wins by roughly 10–20 points. California has not elected a Republican to a statewide office since 2006.
  3. The ~15–20% risk: the Dem field stays fractured through the May 4 VBM mailing and both Republicans advance. In that scenario, Hilton is the likely November winner.

The Democratic field has 34 days to consolidate. The available mechanisms — low-polling Dems dropping out and endorsing, a Newsom or Obama statement (neither forecasted), and Steyer's May broadcast saturation meeting the PG&E-backed counter-PAC — are known. Whether any of them land in time is the open question.

If the November ballot ends up with two Republicans, it will be because seven active Democrats collectively polled under a combined Republican vote share — a math outcome, not a voter preference.

CEO Briefing.

Two-minute read. What matters, what to watch, where the risk is.

Bottom line up front A Democrat is most likely to win the CA governorship in November 2026, but with a meaningful (~15–20%) tail risk of losing the seat because the Democratic field is fragmented enough that top-two-primary math could advance both Republicans. Election June 2, 2026; 34 days remaining.

The asset at stake

The California governor presides over the fourth-largest economy in the world (per the Newsom administration's April 2026 release citing ~$4.25T GDP; Japan slipped to fifth), a $321.9B enacted state budget (2025–26), judicial appointments, and a major voice on federal immigration, environmental, and labor policy. A Republican governor would be the first to hold a statewide office in California since 2006.

The structural issue

California uses a top-two, non-partisan primary. Seven active Democrats are splitting a Democratic-leaning electorate (~45% registered); two Republicans are consolidating a ~25% GOP electorate. On current polling, if no Democrat breaks 15% and both Republicans hold their shares, both top-two slots could go to the GOP.

What's already happened (what's watchable is narrower)

  • April 22 debate (resolved): Six qualified candidates on stage (Hilton, Bianco, Steyer, Becerra, Porter, Mahan); Villaraigosa and Thurmond did not meet the 5% threshold. No clear winner; the race remained static post-debate.
  • April 23 anti-Steyer PAC (resolved, scaled): PG&E launched the public-facing PAC "California Is Not for Sale" via a feeder ("Californians for Resilient and Affordable Energy"); by Apr 27 the broader ad stack reached ~$14M including CA Realtors ($5M), CA BIA ($1M), CCPOA, and IBEW 1245.
  • April 27 CBS News/YouGov (resolved): Hilton 16, Steyer 15, Becerra 13, Bianco 10, Porter 9; 26% undecided. Becerra's third-place finish ahead of Bianco is the cross-pollster confirmation of his post-Swalwell rise. Steyer's self-fund revised up to ~$132M.
  • April 27–28 attack-ad exchange (resolved): Steyer launched a pair of attack ads on Becerra over the HHS unaccompanied-minor record; CHIRLA Action Fund counter-pressed.
  • April 28 second debate (resolved): CBS LA / Pomona College, all eight ballot-qualified candidates on stage under a 1% threshold. Becerra absorbed the heaviest cross-fire; no clear winner.

Three watchable catalysts before June 2

  • May 5 CNN debate: Two-hour special; the next dated catalyst.
  • May 4 VBM mailing: Once ballots are in voters' hands, late consolidation becomes mechanically harder.
  • Newspaper editorial endorsements: LA Times, SF Chronicle, Sacramento Bee, San Diego Union-Tribune, and Fresno Bee had not issued primary endorsements as of April 29; editorial boards typically land 2–3 weeks before VBM — watch the next 10 days.

Three catalysts that change the base case

  • Late Obama or Newsom endorsement. Unlikely but decisive. Would lock a Democrat into the runoff.
  • Second major scandal. A Swalwell-style collapse. The most opposition-research-exposed candidates include Porter (staffer complaints, 2013 court filings), Villaraigosa (FPPC fine, Herbalife), Becerra (HHS follow-up-contact record).
  • Bianco legal event. A federal indictment or DOJ finding on the Riverside pattern-and-practice investigation, or a ruling against him on the ballot seizure, would drop the GOP share and push toward Scenario 3 (D-vs-D).

The two-line takeaway

A Democrat is still the most likely winner, but fragmentation is real and the PG&E PAC changes the money side. The Republican field's ceiling is low in November, but the top-two math makes the June primary the riskier inflection point.

  • Undecided ~23% typically breaks in the final 72 hours — some toward name-ID leaders, some toward late-broadcast saturation, some toward party default.
  • VBM ballots begin mailing May 4. Under universal VBM (2020–present), roughly 80%+ of California votes are cast by mail — the decision window effectively closes ~May 10–15.

ELI13.

You're in 8th grade. You know how elections work. Here's what's weird about this one.

California picks a new governor every four years. Gavin Newsom has been governor for 8 years, which is the maximum — he has to leave in January 2027. So this June, Californians vote in a primary to start picking his replacement.

Here's the twist that makes California different: everyone runs on the same ballot. In most states, Democrats have their own primary and Republicans have their own primary. In California, all 61 candidates — Democrats, Republicans, Greens, everyone — are on one ballot, and whichever two get the most votes advance to the November election. Doesn't matter what party they're from.

Usually this doesn't cause problems because usually one Democrat and one Republican finish top-two, and then in November the Democrat wins because California has almost twice as many registered Democrats as registered Republicans.

But this year is different.

There are seven active Democrats running — a billionaire, ex-members of Congress, mayors, a schools superintendent — and they're splitting the Democratic vote. Nobody is breaking away. The highest-polling Democrat is at 14% (or 23% in one April poll — the race is in flux).

Meanwhile there are only two Republicans running, and they're at 17% and 14%. Higher than most Democrats.

So mathematically, it is possible that in June, both Republicans get more votes than any single Democrat, and they both advance to November. That would mean the November ballot has no Democrat on it at all. And then no matter who Californians pick, the next governor is a Republican. In a state that has not elected a statewide Republican in 20 years.

This is sometimes called "spoiling your own primary." It's happening because the Democrats did not coalesce around one candidate early.

Things shifted in April. The Democrat who had been leading — Eric Swalwell, a congressman from the Bay Area — had five women come forward with allegations of sexual misconduct. He suspended his campaign on April 12 and resigned from Congress, effective April 14. Swalwell has denied the allegations. His supporters are now spread across the other seven Democrats, which deepens the splitting problem.

Also in April, Betty Yee (a former state official) dropped out and endorsed Tom Steyer. And three other Democrats had already dropped out of the race last fall (Kounalakis, Atkins, plus others).

The two Republicans are controversial too.

Steve Hilton is a former Fox News host who worked for the British Prime Minister in the early 2010s. He became an American citizen in 2021. Donald Trump endorsed him on April 6.

Chad Bianco is the sheriff of Riverside County. He confirmed in 2021 that he was a paid member of the Oath Keepers militia in 2014 (some Oath Keepers members were convicted over January 6). His county's jails recorded 18 inmate deaths in 2022 — the most in 15 years — and the California DOJ is investigating his department over jail deaths and use of force. In April 2026, his department seized about 650,000 ballots from a different election on fraud claims; the California Supreme Court halted the seizure and the warrants were unsealed.

These are the two Republicans who could reach the November ballot.

What will probably happen? Most likely — roughly 70–75% chance — one Democrat breaks out of the pack, makes it to November, and beats a Republican in the general. California usually elects Democrats statewide.

But there is a ~15–20% chance the Democrats stay fragmented, both Republicans advance, and the next California governor is a Republican for the first time in 20 years.

That's the story. 34 days until the primary.

ELI5.

You're five. Here's the story with no big words.

California is a big place with a boss called the governor. The boss has to leave soon because he's been boss a long time.

Now people want to be the new boss. One team — the Blue team — has seven people still trying. The other team — the Red team — has only two.

The rule is: everyone counts up their votes, and the top two get to be in the big final. It doesn't matter what team they're on.

California is mostly a Blue state. Usually Blue wins. But this time Blue has too many people wanting the same job, and they're sharing their votes with each other. Nobody has very many.

The two Red team people don't have to share their votes with as many people, so each of them has more.

If the Blue team doesn't pick just one or two people really soon, BOTH Red people might win the top two, and then the new boss HAS to be Red. Even though most Californians are Blue!

Also, the Blue team's best person got in big trouble and had to quit. So now it's even harder for them.

Most grown-ups think Blue will still win. But nobody is sure. The vote is in 34 days.

That's the story.
