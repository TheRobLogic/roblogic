Jump to The Swalwell Collapse The Democratic Field The Republican Field Where They Stand Follow the Money How It Shakes Out The Bottom Line

Gavin Newsom can't run again. That should be the simple headline. California Democrats have a roughly 20-point registration advantage (April 3, 2026 SoS 60-day report: 44.92% D / 25.03% R / 22.76% NPP / 7.30% other — Dems down, Republicans up slightly vs. the Dec 30 154-day report), haven't seen a Republican win a statewide race since 2006, and have multiple plausible succession candidates. In any normal cycle, this is a coronation with a primary speed bump.

But Kamala Harris announced in July 2025 that she would not run, and Sen. Alex Padilla has also declined — removing the two candidates most capable of clearing the Democratic field. As of May 12, 2026 — 21 days from the June 2 primary — the Democratic field is splintered across six active candidates; in independent polling no Democrat has cleared the high teens, while in party- and candidate-commissioned polling Becerra has caught Hilton at the top. The candidate who had been leading in March (Swalwell) resigned from Congress on April 14 after sexual-misconduct allegations, and Hilton has held the cross-pollster lead in every public poll since.

Because California uses a top-two primary — all candidates on one ballot regardless of party; the top two advance to November — there is a real, mathematical scenario in which the November 2026 ballot has no Democrat on it. Paul Mitchell's model put that probability at 27% before Swalwell exited; post-Swalwell estimates from Mitchell and other California strategists now run closer to 15–20%.

With Harris and Padilla on the sidelines and Swalwell out, the Democratic field is a fractured second tier. Gavin Newsom's successor could plausibly be wearing red.

§1The Swalwell Collapse

TL;DR: Between Friday April 10 and Tuesday April 14, Rep. Eric Swalwell went from leading Democratic contender for California governor to a resigned ex-congressman facing a Manhattan DA investigation and a growing roster of accusers. Swalwell has denied the allegations. A fifth accuser came forward publicly on April 15.

Timeline

  • Friday April 10 — San Francisco Chronicle and CNN publish near-simultaneous reports. The Chronicle reports allegations from a former Castro Valley district-office staffer (hired in 2019 at age 21) alleging two non-consensual encounters beginning that year. CNN separately reports allegations from four women, including a 2024 New York hotel rape allegation from a different former staffer, plus three women alleging unsolicited explicit messages and advances. Swalwell calls the reports "flat false."
  • Saturday April 11 — Manhattan DA confirms an investigation into the 2024 New York hotel allegation.
  • Sunday April 12 — Swalwell suspends his campaign: "I am deeply sorry for mistakes in judgment I've made in my past. I will fight the serious, false allegations that have been made — but that's my fight, not a campaign's." An open letter signed by 50+ former staffers calls the reporting "serious, credible" and demands he resign from Congress.
  • Monday April 13 — Swalwell announces he will resign from Congress.
  • Tuesday April 14 — Resignation effective. The House Ethics Committee had opened an inquiry that same week but closed it upon his resignation (the committee has no jurisdiction over former members).
  • Tuesday April 15 — At a Los Angeles press conference (covered by AP, the LA Times, and CNN), Lonna Drewes alleges Swalwell drugged and raped her at a West Hollywood hotel in 2018 under the pretense of retrieving "papers." Her attorney says more women have made contact. Swalwell's legal team denies the allegation. The LA County Sheriff's Department opened a rape investigation tied to the Drewes allegation on April 14 (CNBC).
  • Monday April 20 — The House Ethics Committee issued a rare public statement defending its record on sexual-misconduct probes and inviting additional alleged victims to come forward — unusual given the committee has no further jurisdiction over Swalwell.

The Collapse

Within hours of the Friday reports, the support floor dropped out. Campaign chair Rep. Jimmy Gomez resigned his role and publicly called for Swalwell to exit. Sens. Gallego, Schiff, Padilla pulled support Friday afternoon. Reps. Ted Lieu, Doris Matsui, Mike Thompson rescinded endorsements. The California Teachers Association and SEIU California suspended their backing. Pelosi reportedly called Swalwell on Sunday urging resignation.

Ballot Reality Swalwell suspended after the statutory withdrawal deadline, so his name will still appear on the June 2 ballot. Any votes he receives are effectively wasted — a real risk given the compressed timeline and vote-by-mail ballots beginning to mail May 4.

Prior Baggage, for Context

Fang Fang (2020): Swalwell had a prolonged association with Christine Fang, a suspected Chinese intelligence operative who cultivated Bay Area politicians from his Dublin city council days and helped bundle for his early House campaigns. The FBI gave him a defensive briefing in 2015; a House Ethics probe closed in May 2023 with no action. In March 2026, FBI Director Kash Patel pushed to release the old investigative file; Swalwell's legal team responded publicly opposing release.

Trump impeachment manager (2020): Swalwell served as one of the House managers in the second Trump impeachment trial, which raised his national profile and made him a persistent Trump-administration target — context for Patel's March 2026 push on the Fang files.

Rep. Sam Liccardo (D-CA) told ABC7 on April 14 that rumors of Swalwell's misconduct had "swirled for years" in California political circles.

Sources: CNN · NPR · WaPo · CalMatters · SF Standard

§2The Democratic Field

California's bench was supposed to be the deepest in the country. Harris and Padilla opted out; Swalwell collapsed; Kounalakis, Atkins, and Yee have since withdrawn. What remains is a field of six active candidates. In independent polls (SurveyUSA, Kreate) the highest-polling Democrat is Steyer in the high teens; in party- and candidate-commissioned polls (CADEM/EVITARUS, Impact, Mellman) Becerra has tied Hilton at the top.

Tom SteyerD · 14–18%

Hedge-fund founder, climate-first populist. Third statewide campaign; his 2020 presidential run spent ~$253M (mostly self-funded) for no delegates.

Record: ~$147M self-funded per the CalMatters May filings tracker — now the second-most-expensive CA gubernatorial campaign in history (behind Whitman 2010 $176M). Per published reporting and SEC filings, Farallon Capital — which he founded and left in 2012 — held positions valued at roughly $90M in CoreCivic (private ICE detention) during his tenure; he has called that "a mistake." Farallon also financed $2B+ in Indonesian and Australian coal acquisitions through ~2014. Steyer's public climate advocacy began ~2012 after he left Farallon. Apr 23 → May 7: the anti-Steyer ad stack grew from ~$14M to ~$26M+ (PG&E feeder ~$11.5M, JOBSPAC/CalChamber $6.5M, CA Real Estate IE $7M, CA BIA $1M, CCPOA, IBEW 1245). May 5: Steyer filed an FPPC complaint alleging PG&E illegally funneled ~$8M through a shell to disguise itself as the largest funder and that ads failed required top-funder disclosures. Endorsements (post-Apr 29): SEIU California (week of Apr 30) — resolving the largest outstanding labor question after CTA's Apr 10 flip. At the May 5 CNN debate, Hilton attacked: "If you think it can't get worse in California, I've got two words: Tom Steyer." Steyer reaffirmed Medicare-for-All as "the only way."

Attack-ad raw material: coal and CoreCivic footage; Atherton hedge-fund biography cutting against populist framing.

Katie PorterD · 8–9%

Whiteboard progressive. Finished third in the 2024 Senate top-two (behind Schiff and Garvey) — did not make the runoff.

Record: Per 2013 court filings, her then-husband Matthew Hoffman alleged Porter threw a bowl of mashed potatoes at him; Porter denied that framing, alleged physical abuse, and obtained a restraining order. Both parties sought protective orders. Former staffer Sasha Georgiades (Navy veteran) has publicly alleged Porter's office dismissed a sexual-harassment complaint; Porter's campaign has disputed the account. May 7 CalMatters profile: Q1 fundraising under $3M over four months (below pace), two viral "yelling" videos surfaced in early May (a staffer and a reporter), establishment defection to Becerra/Steyer/Villaraigosa. Porter released a self-deprecating response ad. May 4: McClatchy CA editorial board (Sacramento Bee, Fresno Bee, Modesto Bee, Merced Sun-Star, San Luis Obispo Tribune) endorsed Porter — first major newspaper endorsement of the cycle. May 2026: Sen. Elizabeth Warren TV ad calling Porter "the governor California needs" began airing (endorsement was February; ad buy is new). At the May 5 CNN debate, asked about the staff-yelling clip she framed it as accountability; quipped "Boys, boys, enough with the bickering."

Attack-ad raw material: already rejected statewide once; recent viral yelling clips.

Xavier BecerraD · 10–23% cross-poll

Biden's HHS Secretary; former CA AG. Biggest gainer from the Swalwell exit. Polling picture is genuinely split: SurveyUSA Apr 28–May 1 (independent): 10% · CADEM EVITARUS Apr 30–May 2: 18%, tied for first with Hilton · Impact Research Apr 28–May 3 (Becerra-aligned): 23%, tied with Hilton · Mellman May 3–6 (Mahan-IE-aligned): 20%, tied with Hilton · Kreate May 5–9 (independent): 20%, second to Hilton. Opponents raised a former chief-of-staff (Sean McCluskie) guilty plea over campaign funds, and a reported Medicare-for-All flip-flop; Becerra denied involvement in the McCluskie matter and reaffirmed M4A. Hilton: "mired, personally, in a corruption scandal." Becerra to Hilton: "Donald Trump's his daddy." May 7: Hilton publicly accused CHIRLA (a Becerra endorser) of using undocumented canvassers for the campaign and questioned $72M in public funding to the group. May 11: CHIRLA Action Fund held a press conference defending Becerra. May 12: a KTLA interview generated the day's clip — Becerra opened with "is this a profile, not a gotcha?" and on the missing-minors record said "some of these kids, probably because they needed to earn some money, started working in places that were very exploitive." No on-camera follow-up issued. Newsom signal: CNN May 8 reported members of Newsom's political team are consulting and fundraising for Becerra (Newsom himself has not endorsed).

Record: Per New York Times 2023–24 reporting, HHS failed to re-contact ~85,000 unaccompanied migrant children placed with sponsors during his tenure, with documented child-labor outcomes at some sponsor addresses; House Homeland Security subpoenaed him in September 2024. Former LA mayor. Got 13% against Newsom in the 2018 primary. ~$6.96M cumulative per April state filings (~$707K raised in the four months ending mid-April) — top direct-fundraising Dem after Steyer at the February cut, slowing into spring. SurveyUSA Apr 28–May 1 had him at 5%.

Record: 2007 Mirthala Salinas affair ended his marriage. Paid advisor to Herbalife — an MLM the League of United Latin American Citizens publicly condemned him over. FPPC fined him $41,849 in 2024 for 21 undisclosed gifts (Dodgers, Shakira, Aretha, Grammys, Globes) — the largest FPPC ethics fine ever levied against an individual California elected official at the time. In 2018, charter-school independent-expenditure money (Reed Hastings $7M, Eli Broad $2.5M, Bloomberg $2M) dumped ~$22M into his losing bid. Same donor cluster is active this cycle. Qualified for the May 5 CNN debate (3% threshold); confronted Bianco directly on the 2014 Oath Keepers membership and pivoted to attack Becerra's HHS record.

Attack-ad raw material: Herbalife advisory role; FPPC fine; 2018 loss.

Mayor of San Jose. Year three of his term, attempting to skip straight to governor.

With Harris and Padilla opting out, and Kounalakis, Atkins, Yee, and Swalwell withdrawing or suspending, California Democrats face a second-tier primary — six active candidates, with the Democratic frontrunner contested across pollsters.

§3The Republican Field

California has not elected a Republican to a statewide office since 2006, but the top-two primary makes intra-GOP math the story of the moment. The two GOP candidates are former Fox News host Steve Hilton and Riverside Sheriff Chad Bianco. Neither cleared the 60% threshold at the April 12 CAGOP convention (Bianco 49, Hilton 44). At current polling, they are the best-polling candidates in the field.

Steve HiltonR · 20% (lead)

Former Fox News host and British political consultant. Spent 11 years at Fox ("The Next Revolution," 2017–2023) after emigrating from the UK. Lives in Atherton, CA.

Record: David Cameron's Director of Strategy 2010–2012 (the "Big Society" / Brexit-era Downing Street). Became a US citizen in May 2021; held dual UK/US citizenship 2021–2024; renounced UK citizenship in 2024 (£492 fee). Wife Rachel Whetstone is CCO at Netflix (ex-Google, ex-Uber). Book: Positive Populism. UK political memoirs describe him as Cameron's "pint-sized Rasputin."

Fundraising: Top Republican fundraiser at ~$7.1M total raised through ~18,000 individual contributions (per April 2026 CalMatters/KRON4 campaign reports; the frequently-cited "30,000 donors" figure reflects the February snapshot). Per CalMatters, Hilton has also self-loaned approximately $200K to his campaign. Sergey Brin contributed the $39,200 individual maximum.

The Trump wrinkle: Trump endorsed him April 6. Six days later, the CAGOP convention declined to endorse — 44% Hilton, 49% Bianco, neither hitting the 60% threshold. Hilton embraced the endorsement at the April 22 debate ("a deep honor") and pitched a "constructive relationship" with the Trump administration on forestry, energy, and fraud. Polling consolidation: from CBS/YouGov Apr 23–27 16% to SurveyUSA Apr 28–May 1 20% to Kreate May 5–9 22% — Hilton holds first across every public poll since April.

May 5 CNN debate: on Steyer, "If you think it can't get worse in California, I've got two words: Tom Steyer." On Becerra, "mired, personally, in a corruption scandal." Declined to say Trump lost 2020 when pressed by moderators. May 7: opened a new front, publicly accusing CHIRLA (a Becerra endorser) of using undocumented canvassers for the Becerra campaign and questioning $72M in public funding to the group.

General-election headwinds: Republicans haven't won statewide since 2006; a ~20-point Dem registration advantage; national-origin rhetoric used against him at CAGOP convention by Bianco allies.

Chad BiancoR · 12–14%

Riverside County Sheriff. Campaigning on a "law and order" platform. CJCJ reports his department recorded the lowest Part 1 crime-clearance rate of any California sheriff's department from 2019–2024 (9.2% — less than half the state average).

Record: Riverside County jails recorded 18 inmate deaths in 2022, the highest number in 15 years per LAist reporting. The department is under a California DOJ pattern-and-practice investigation opened in February 2023 over jail deaths and use-of-force; no new public milestone Apr 29–May 12. A 2014 Oath Keepers membership ($40, one-year) was confirmed by Bianco after a 2021 data leak; he has said he quickly let the membership lapse. In April 2026, Bianco's department seized approximately 650,000 ballots tied to California's redistricting referendum on election-fraud grounds; the California Supreme Court halted the action and unsealed warrants that did not cite direct evidence of fraud. On April 14, the Riverside County Board of Supervisors voted 4–1 to decline paying the department's legal defense. In late April, the CA Supreme Court granted AG Bonta's request for an expedited briefing schedule. As of May 12, no new CA Supreme Court order has issued; no oral argument date is posted; no federal indictment has been filed. Bianco endorsed Trump in 2024 in uniform on Instagram ("It's time we put a felon in the White House") — video still circulating.

May 5 CNN debate: when Villaraigosa pressed him on Oath Keepers, Bianco doubled down — "proud Oath Keeper." Becerra accused him of unlawfully seizing 650,000+ ballots during the 2025 referendum.

Fundraising: ~$4.4M cumulative per CA SoS filings, with donor appeals for legal defense. Cumulative raise now trails Hilton's by roughly 2:1.

General-election headwinds: the 650K-ballot seizure footage; DOJ pattern-and-practice investigation; general-election math against a statewide Democratic registration advantage.

§6How It Shakes Out

Three scenarios for the June 2 top-two primary. Probabilities as of May 12, 21 days out, with most VBM ballots already in voters' hands. Numbers are ranges from public polling (SurveyUSA, Kreate, CBS/YouGov, Emerson, EVITARUS, Impact, Mellman) and Paul Mitchell's model, not a single forecaster.

Scenario 1 · R-vs-R November ballot

~12–18% probability

Both Hilton and Bianco finish top-two. The Dem field stays fractured. Combined GOP share rises from current ~32–34% to 35–37% via late undecided consolidation in the Central Valley and Inland Empire. Probability has tightened since Apr 29 — Becerra's surge in Dem-aligned polling and the McClatchy endorsement of Porter both narrow the path, but Hilton's cross-pollster consolidation at ~20% keeps it live.

What pushes toward this: The Becerra May 5–12 stretch (McCluskie attack, KTLA "needed to earn some money" clip, CHIRLA fight) damaging him without lifting Steyer; Porter's collapse continuing; further low-polling-Dem holdouts (Villaraigosa, Mahan, Thurmond) refusing to drop; Bianco's CA Supreme Court case being delayed past June 2.

November dynamics: California has not elected a Republican to a statewide office since 2006. In this scenario the November race becomes a GOP ideology contest (Hilton-Trump-aligned vs. Bianco-Oath-Keeper). Dem turnout would likely decline, which would pressure down-ballot House districts (CA-22, CA-27, CA-41, CA-45). Head-to-head, public polling averages suggest Hilton would beat Bianco comfortably.

Scenario 2 · R-vs-D November ballot (most likely)

~72–78% probability

Hilton consolidates the GOP to 20–23% and finishes first. One Democrat breaks from the pack to 16–20% and takes second. Bianco lands third at 12–14%. As of May 12, the Dem second-place lane is a Steyer-vs-Becerra contest — independent polls (SurveyUSA, Kreate) still favor Steyer; party- and candidate-commissioned polls (CADEM/EVITARUS, Impact, Mellman) favor Becerra. Porter has been demoted from the contest by the May 7 CalMatters profile and the May 5 debate.

Which Democrat? Steyer retains the money advantage (~$147M self-fund, CTA + SEIU CA endorsements, decisive broadcast capacity against the $26M+ anti-Steyer stack); Becerra has the institutional and party-poll momentum (26 state-lawmaker endorsements, Newsom-team operatives consulting/fundraising). The May 5 debate handed Becerra the "consensus target" label, which the May 7 Hilton-CHIRLA front and the May 12 KTLA clip have compounded. Whether that damage shows up in next week's independent polls is the open question.

Catalysts already resolved: April 22 + April 28 debates. April 23 → May 7 anti-Steyer ad stack scaled to $26M+. April 30 SEIU California endorsement of Steyer. May 4 McClatchy editorial board endorsement of Porter. May 4 VBM mailing — earliest ballots in voters' hands. Catalysts still open: LA Times, SF Chronicle (gov pick paywalled), and SDUT editorial endorsements. Whether Becerra's debate-week damage shows up in independent polls (next SurveyUSA / Berkeley IGS / PPIC). Any Bianco CA Supreme Court ruling or federal action before June 2. Scenario 3 · D-vs-D November ballot

~4–10% probability

Simultaneous decline by both Republicans. Combined GOP share falls from ~32–34% to 22–24%. Two Democrats consolidate into the high teens — likeliest pairing Steyer (~18%) + Becerra (~17%).

What pushes toward this: A federal indictment or civil finding against Bianco; a CA Supreme Court ruling against him before VBM returns are tabulated; CAGOP Bianco delegates visibly defecting from Hilton; a late Newsom or Obama endorsement (unlikely but decisive — Newsom reiterated "no primary" on May 8).

November dynamics: A policy contest between Steyer's wealth-tax populism and Becerra's institutional experience. Republican turnout would likely crater. Head-to-head, Becerra probably holds a slight edge (Steyer's self-funder profile cuts against populist framing in a one-on-one).

The cross-cutting factors

Trump's Hilton endorsement splits, doesn't consolidate. The convention vote (Bianco 49, Hilton 44) is the tell: Bianco's base sees Hilton as a carpetbagging Fox personality, and Trump's thumb on the scale activated anti-establishment GOP grievance. Net effect: +3–5 pts for Hilton with MAGA-aligned indies, −2–3 pts with Bianco-loyal grassroots. Helps Hilton finish first, hurts combined GOP total. The May 5 debate moment where Hilton declined to say Trump lost 2020 reinforces this dynamic.

Unifying Dem endorsement is not coming. Newsom's April 15 statement, April 28 WaPo signal ("supporting a Democrat in the runoff"), and May 8 reiteration ("it's my intention not to get involved in the primary") all explicitly declined to endorse before June 2. CNN May 8 reported Newsom's political team is consulting and fundraising for Becerra — a soft signal that falls short of a formal endorsement. Historical precedent: Brown stayed out of 2018 Newsom-Villaraigosa. Absence is priced in. A late Obama nod would lock Scenario 2 and decide which Democrat advances. Don't bet on it.

Turnout. Non-presidential June primaries run 35–40%, skewing older, whiter, higher-propensity — which helps Republicans and hurts fragmented Democrats. Undecideds compressed from ~26% in late April to 14–17% by mid-May; late breakers will skew toward name-ID + TV saturation. Steyer's self-funding is the clearest late-break advantage; Hilton's Fox tenure is second. The "hold your VBM ballot" social-media push from some Democratic operators is the cycle's new turnout wrinkle — AG Bonta called it "potentially unlawful" misinformation on May 8.

§7The Bottom Line

The most likely outcome, consensus view as of May 12:

  1. June 2 primary: Hilton finishes first (~20–22%). Steyer or Becerra finishes second (~16–20%). Bianco third (~12–14%).
  2. November general: A Democrat wins by roughly 10–20 points. California has not elected a Republican to a statewide office since 2006.
  3. The ~12–18% risk: the Dem field stays fractured through VBM tabulation and both Republicans advance. In that scenario, Hilton is the likely November winner.

The Democratic field has 21 days to consolidate, but most of the consolidation mechanisms have already played out: SEIU and CTA are with Steyer; Speaker Rivas + 26 state lawmakers and the Newsom team's quiet support are with Becerra; McClatchy is with Porter; Newsom himself has explicitly stayed out. The remaining variables are independent-poll confirmation of Becerra's surge (or non-confirmation), whether any of the remaining newspaper editorial boards (LA Times, SF Chronicle, SDUT) shift the lane, and whether Becerra's May 5–12 stretch (McCluskie, CHIRLA, KTLA) leaves a mark on his cross-pollster average.

If the November ballot ends up with two Republicans, it will be because six active Democrats collectively polled under a combined Republican vote share — a math outcome, not a voter preference.

The Hilton+Steyer vs. Hilton+Becerra split now depends on which pollster you weight — independent polls favor Steyer second-place, Dem-aligned polls favor Becerra.

ELI13.

You're in 8th grade. You know how elections work. Here's what's weird about this one.

California picks a new governor every four years. Gavin Newsom has been governor for 8 years, which is the maximum — he has to leave in January 2027. So this June, Californians vote in a primary to start picking his replacement.

Here's the twist that makes California different: everyone runs on the same ballot. In most states, Democrats have their own primary and Republicans have their own primary. In California, all 61 candidates — Democrats, Republicans, Greens, everyone — are on one ballot, and whichever two get the most votes advance to the November election. Doesn't matter what party they're from.

Usually this doesn't cause problems because usually one Democrat and one Republican finish top-two, and then in November the Democrat wins because California has almost twice as many registered Democrats as registered Republicans.

But this year is different.

There are six active Democrats running — a billionaire, an ex-Cabinet secretary, ex-members of Congress, mayors, a schools superintendent — and they're splitting the Democratic vote. The Democratic frontrunner depends on which poll you trust: independent broadcast polls have Tom Steyer in the high teens with Xavier Becerra in single digits; Dem-party and candidate-aligned polls have Becerra tied for first with the top Republican.

Meanwhile there are only two Republicans running. Steve Hilton (former Fox News host) leads the cross-pollster average at ~20%; Chad Bianco (Riverside sheriff) is at ~12–14%. Hilton's number is higher than any Democrat's in independent polls.

So mathematically, it is still possible that in June, both Republicans get more votes than any single Democrat, and they both advance to November. That would mean the November ballot has no Democrat on it at all. And then no matter who Californians pick, the next governor is a Republican. In a state that has not elected a statewide Republican in 20 years.

This is sometimes called "spoiling your own primary." It's happening because the Democrats did not coalesce around one candidate early.

Things shifted in April. The Democrat who had been leading — Eric Swalwell, a congressman from the Bay Area — had five women come forward with allegations of sexual misconduct. He suspended his campaign on April 12 and resigned from Congress, effective April 14. Swalwell has denied the allegations. His supporters are now spread across the other six Democrats, which deepens the splitting problem.

Also in April, Betty Yee (a former state official) dropped out and endorsed Tom Steyer. And three other Democrats had already dropped out of the race last fall (Kounalakis, Atkins, plus others).

What changed since April 29: mail ballots went out May 4. There was one more debate on May 5 (CNN); Becerra became the main target after a former chief of staff pleaded guilty to campaign-fund theft. A Becerra TV interview on May 12 went badly. The biggest labor union still on the sidelines (SEIU California) endorsed Steyer. McClatchy's California newspapers endorsed Katie Porter. The anti-Steyer ad campaign almost doubled to $26M. Gavin Newsom said again on May 8 he won't endorse before June 2.

The two Republicans are controversial too.

Steve Hilton is a former Fox News host who worked for the British Prime Minister in the early 2010s. He became an American citizen in 2021. Donald Trump endorsed him on April 6.

Chad Bianco is the sheriff of Riverside County. He confirmed in 2021 that he was a paid member of the Oath Keepers militia in 2014 (some Oath Keepers members were convicted over January 6). His county's jails recorded 18 inmate deaths in 2022 — the most in 15 years — and the California DOJ is investigating his department over jail deaths and use of force. In April 2026, his department seized about 650,000 ballots from a different election on fraud claims; the California Supreme Court halted the seizure and the warrants were unsealed.

These are the two Republicans who could reach the November ballot.

What will probably happen? Most likely — roughly 70–75% chance — one Democrat breaks out of the pack, makes it to November, and beats a Republican in the general. California usually elects Democrats statewide.

But there is a ~15–20% chance the Democrats stay fragmented, both Republicans advance, and the next California governor is a Republican for the first time in 20 years.

That's the story. 21 days until the primary, and most ballots are already in voters' hands.

ELI5.

You're five. Here's the story with no big words.

California is a big place with a boss called the governor. The boss has to leave soon because he's been boss a long time.

Now people want to be the new boss. One team — the Blue team — has six people still trying. The other team — the Red team — has only two.

The rule is: everyone counts up their votes, and the top two get to be in the big final. It doesn't matter what team they're on.

California is mostly a Blue state. Usually Blue wins. But this time Blue has too many people wanting the same job, and they're sharing their votes with each other. Nobody has very many.

The two Red team people don't have to share their votes with as many people, so each of them has more.

If the Blue team doesn't pick just one or two people really soon, BOTH Red people might win the top two, and then the new boss HAS to be Red. Even though most Californians are Blue!

Also, the Blue team's best person got in big trouble and had to quit. So now it's even harder for them.

Most grown-ups think Blue will still win. But nobody is sure. The vote is in 21 days. People can already mail in their answers — most have their ballot already.

That's the story.
