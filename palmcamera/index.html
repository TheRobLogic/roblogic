Watch Out For

Single point of failure.

The UDM-SE is your router, switch, NVR, and controller in one box. If it dies, everything goes down — network, cameras, all of it.

Power supply failures are a known issue.

The internal PSU can stop outputting 54V. Ubiquiti won't sell replacements and won't fix out of warranty. Always run it on a UPS.

4GB RAM, single drive bay.

The UDM Pro Max ($599) has 8GB RAM, dual drive bays with RAID, and double the device limit. $100 more for meaningfully better hardware. Worth considering.

WiFi version is end-of-life, sold out everywhere. The G6 Entry Doorbell ($249, PoE) is the current replacement and actually in stock.

G4 Instant has no smart detection

No person/vehicle/animal detection — motion only. It's also WiFi, which means it can be jammed and will drop frames. We dropped it for an all-wired setup.

UDM-SE needs a WiFi AP

The UDM-SE has zero WiFi capability. The original list didn't account for this — you'd have no wireless for your phone, laptop, or tablet without adding an access point.

Added a 3rd bullet camera

Two outdoor cameras leaves blind spots. Three covers front approach, side yard, and rear — full perimeter for a house with two doors and open yard.

Things to Know Applies to all three options

Don't put the router in the attic.

Attics hit 120°F+ in summer. All UniFi consoles are rated to 104°F max. Put it in a closet or conditioned space. Run one cable from the modem in the attic down to it, then cables back up and out to cameras.

PoE doorbell means running Cat6 to the front door.

All current UniFi doorbells are PoE — no WiFi versions available. That means a cable run to the door, not reusing existing doorbell wiring. Factor this into the installer's scope.

Pin your firmware versions.

UniFi firmware updates have bricked devices. Turn off auto-update and wait for community confirmation before upgrading. This applies to both the router and camera firmware.

Ubiquiti account required for initial setup.

You need a cloud account to configure the router the first time. After that, create a local-only admin account and disable cloud access. Ubiquiti had a major data breach in 2021 — minimize your cloud exposure.

Smart detection is hit or miss.

Person, vehicle, and animal detection works but expect false positives from shadows and tree movement. It's useful, just not perfect.

Home Assistant integration breaks on updates.

Both HA and Protect firmware updates can break the integration. Manageable but annoying — pin versions on both sides if you want stability.

Hiring an Installer

What you're hiring for: 4–5 Cat6 cable runs from a central closet to exterior camera mounts (and one to the front door for the doorbell), plus camera mounting and live testing. Expect $600–1,200 depending on difficulty.
  • Must have a California C-7 Low Voltage license — verify at cslb.ca.gov
  • Ask for proof of liability insurance and workers' comp
  • Insist on solid copper Cat6 (not copper-clad aluminum / CCA)
  • They should test every run with a proper cable tester, not just "plug and check"
  • Exterior penetrations need drip loops and silicone sealant
  • Have them mount the cameras too — they own the whole job, test live on-site
  • Mark camera locations with painter's tape before they arrive
  • Have cameras on hand so they can test adoption into Protect on-site
  • Ask them to leave service loops (6–10 ft of extra cable) in the attic at each end
