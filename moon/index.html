Mission Overview

NASA's Artemis program is humanity's return to the Moon, more than half a century after Apollo. Named for the twin sister of Apollo in Greek mythology, the program aims to land the first woman and the first person of color on the lunar surface, establish a sustained human presence, and use the Moon as a proving ground for the technologies needed to send astronauts to Mars. The program uses the Space Launch System (SLS) — the most powerful rocket ever built — paired with the Orion crew capsule designed for deep space missions far beyond low Earth orbit.

Artemis I proved the hardware in November–December 2022, sending an uncrewed Orion spacecraft around the Moon on a 25.5-day journey that took it farther from Earth than any spacecraft designed for humans has ever traveled. Artemis II, launched April 1, 2026, carried four astronauts on the first crewed flight around the Moon since Apollo 17 in 1972 — including the first Canadian to travel beyond low Earth orbit. During their lunar flyby on April 6, the crew spent seven hours photographing the Moon's surface, captured a stunning new Earthrise, and witnessed a total solar eclipse from the far side of the Moon: an event never before seen by human eyes.
